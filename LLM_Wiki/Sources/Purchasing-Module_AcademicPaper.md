**INTRODUCTION**

**1.1 Background of the Study**

The global agricultural supply chain remains one of the most critical yet fragile components of food production systems worldwide. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (2023), agricultural input supply chains encompass the provisioning of fertilizers, pesticides, seeds, and animal feeds that are essential for sustaining agricultural productivity. In developing economies, these supply chains are often characterized by fragmentation, limited digital infrastructure, and a heavy reliance on manual processes that impede operational efficiency. The purchasing and procurement functions within agricultural supply businesses, in particular, have been identified as areas where digital transformation can yield significant improvements in cost control, supplier management, and inventory optimization (Van Weele, 2018). As global markets increasingly demand transparency and traceability in supply chains, the need for systematic and data-driven purchasing operations becomes not merely advantageous but essential for business survival and competitiveness.

In the Philippines, the agricultural sector remains a cornerstone of the national economy, contributing approximately 9.5 percent to the country's gross domestic product and employing nearly a quarter of the labor force (Philippine Statistics Authority, 2023). Agricultural supply stores, which serve as the primary distributors of farming inputs to rural communities, play a pivotal role in ensuring that smallholder farmers have timely access to essential materials. However, the operational practices of these supply stores have remained largely traditional, with many still relying on handwritten ledgers, verbal agreements with suppliers, and manual shelf inspections to manage their purchasing activities. The absence of purpose-built information systems for procurement means that reorder decisions are frequently based on intuition rather than data, leading to instances of both overstocking and stockouts that directly affect profitability and farmer satisfaction. These challenges are compounded by the inherent volatility of agricultural input prices, which fluctuate in response to global commodity markets, exchange rate movements, and seasonal demand patterns.

At the local level, Villon Farm Supply, an agricultural supply and trade business operating in a rural community in the Philippines, exemplifies these operational challenges. The business serves local farmers with a catalog of approximately 50 distinct products, including fertilizers, pesticides, seeds, and animal feeds. Currently, Villon Farm Supply operates without a purpose-built information system, relying instead on physical record books, manual shelf inspections, informal credit ledgers, and verbal supplier communication. Through preliminary observation and informal interviews, six critical problems have been identified in the purchasing operations of the business. First, reorder decisions are based entirely on gut feel, with no data-driven threshold to trigger procurement activities. Second, the end-to-end purchasing process is fully manual, leaving no audit trail for accountability or compliance purposes. Third, supplier price changes force manual and error-prone retail price updates. Fourth, there is no formal accounts payable ledger to track obligations to suppliers. Fifth, price volatility creates retail pricing errors and incorrect cost of goods sold calculations. Sixth, seasonal demand spikes are not proactively planned, resulting in lost sales opportunities during peak periods.

These six problems collectively represent a significant gap in the operational capabilities of Villon Farm Supply, one that is not unique to this single establishment but is representative of the broader challenges faced by small and medium agricultural supply businesses throughout the country. While enterprise resource planning systems exist for large corporations, the cost, complexity, and infrastructure requirements of such systems render them impractical for businesses of this scale. The gap, therefore, lies in the absence of an affordable, purpose-built, and locally contextualized digital solution that can address the specific purchasing challenges of small agricultural supply operations in the Philippine setting. This study proposes to fill that gap through the development of the Villon Integrated Supply and Trade Application (VISTA), specifically its Purchasing Module, which will be designed as a Windows Presentation Foundation (WPF) client-server desktop application using VB.NET 10, with a MariaDB central database and SQLite offline-first architecture.

What makes this study relevant beyond the walls of Villon Farm Supply is the representativeness of its problems. Gut-feel reorder decisions, absent audit trails, manual price updates, informal AP tracking, and the absence of seasonal demand planning are not idiosyncratic to one store — they describe the purchasing operations of the majority of small agricultural supply businesses throughout the Philippines. A purchasing module that directly addresses all six of these problems, developed on a standard desktop platform without requiring cloud infrastructure or internet dependency, and evaluated against both ISO 25010:2023 and UTAUT2, provides the sector with something it currently lacks: a tested and documented reference for how digital procurement transformation can work at the small-business scale, and what it looks like when it is measured rigorously.

**1.2 Conceptual Framework**

The conceptual framework of this study organizes its variables around the gap between how purchasing decisions are currently made at Villon Farm Supply and how they should be made with the support of a digital system. The independent variables are the six identified manual purchasing problems (P1 through P6) and the practices that generated them: reorder decisions made by visual shelf inspection and intuition (P1), a purchasing workflow that leaves no audit trail (P2), retail prices updated manually after each supplier price change (P3), accounts payable managed informally by supplier visit schedule (P4), price volatility that causes COGS distortions (P5), and the absence of any seasonal demand model for proactive stock-up planning (P6). The intervening variable is the development of the VISTA Purchasing Module — the design, coding, testing, and refinement of the software using WPF and VB.NET 10 — which will translate the identified problems into specific system features. The dependent variables are the outcomes measured at the end of the study: the module’s compliance with ISO 25010:2023 across eight quality characteristics, and its acceptability and usability as measured by the UTAUT2 constructs of user acceptance and perceived ease of use and usefulness as efficacy.

The relationship among these variables is grounded in systems development theory and the iterative relationship between problem identification and solution development. The descriptive component of the study establishes the baseline conditions by identifying and documenting the practices, issues, and problems in the existing manual purchasing system. These findings then serve as input to the developmental component, which translates the identified problems into specific system requirements and interventions. For instance, the problem of gut-feel reorder decisions (P1) is addressed through the development of a reorder suggestion engine that calculates reorder points based on historical consumption data and lead times. Similarly, the absence of audit trails (P2) is addressed through the implementation of a formal purchase order lifecycle that records every transaction from creation to closure. The theoretical foundation for this approach draws from the systems development life cycle (SDLC) methodology, which posits that effective information systems are those that are developed in direct response to clearly identified user requirements and organizational needs (Dennis et al., 2019). Additionally, the evaluation framework draws from the ISO/IEC 25010 quality model, which provides a comprehensive taxonomy of software quality characteristics that can be used to assess the fitness of a developed system for its intended purpose.

The paradigm of this study follows the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model, which provides a structured representation of how the study will transform existing problems into a functional solution. On the input side, the paradigm encompasses the current manual purchasing practices of Villon Farm Supply, the six identified problems (P1 through P6) that characterize those practices, and the business requirements derived from the analysis of those problems. On the process side, the paradigm represents the development of the VISTA Purchasing Module, which includes the creation of a vendor directory for centralized supplier management, a purchase order management system for end-to-end PO lifecycle tracking, a goods receiving module for recording deliveries and verifying orders, an accounts payable tracking system for formalizing financial obligations, a price change detection mechanism for automatic retail price updates, and a reorder suggestion engine for data-driven procurement decisions. On the output side, the paradigm yields a fully digital purchasing system with automated workflows, comprehensive audit trails, data-driven reorder decisions, and formal accounts payable tracking. The evaluation of this output against ISO 25010:2023 standards and UTAUT2 acceptability criteria then provides the basis for determining the quality and acceptability of the developed system.

**1.4 Significance of the Study**

**Business Owners** of agricultural supply stores will benefit from this study through a concrete demonstration of how the financial blind spots created by informal purchasing can be addressed through purposeful system design. At Villon Farm Supply, the owner currently has no formal view of outstanding supplier obligations, no record of how purchase prices have changed across orders, and no basis for anticipating the cost increases that seasonal demand spikes will trigger. The VISTA Purchasing Module changes this by maintaining a vendor directory with pricing history, a formal accounts payable ledger with due-date alerts, and a reorder suggestion engine that incorporates historical consumption and seasonal patterns. Owners who adopt similar systems will move from purchasing based on intuition and supplier trust toward purchasing grounded in data — a shift with direct implications for margin management and supplier negotiating leverage.

**Store Managers** will benefit most visibly in the areas where the manual system currently places the heaviest burden. The current purchasing workflow requires the manager to physically inspect shelves, mentally compare observed stock against recalled minimum levels, call a supplier to place an order, write the order details in a physical book, and then manually update the retail selling price if the supplier quotes a different price than last time. Each of these steps is an opportunity for error and a drain on time that could be spent elsewhere. The VISTA Purchasing Module will replace this sequence with a reorder suggestion engine that calculates when and how much to order, a formal purchase order interface that records every order digitally, and an automated price change alert that notifies the manager to review retail pricing before the new stock is placed for sale.

**Suppliers** will benefit from a more professional and structured ordering process. The implementation of formal purchase orders with complete specifications, agreed-upon pricing, and documented delivery schedules will reduce misunderstandings and disputes between Villon Farm Supply and its suppliers. The vendor directory feature will also facilitate better communication and relationship management, creating a more transparent and predictable procurement environment that benefits both parties in the supply chain.

**Future Researchers** will find this study useful as a methodologically documented case of purchasing system development for an agricultural retail context that has received limited coverage in the information systems literature. The particular combination of features — a reorder suggestion engine that incorporates seasonal demand flags, a price change detection mechanism that alerts on margin impact, and a formal AP tracking system that replaces supplier-visit-based payment scheduling — addresses problems that are common in the sector but rarely treated together in a single system evaluation. The ISO 25010:2023 compliance results and UTAUT2 acceptance scores will provide baseline empirical data, and the documented limitations of this study — particularly the exclusion of machine learning forecasting and EDI integration — will point toward productive directions for future investigations in agricultural procurement digitalization.

**Agricultural Retail Industry** will benefit from this study as a documented proof of concept that purchasing digitalization is feasible at the small business scale without requiring expensive enterprise software or cloud infrastructure. The VISTA Purchasing Module will run on a standard Windows desktop, operate offline-first, and cover the full purchasing lifecycle from reorder suggestion through vendor management, PO creation, goods receiving, and AP tracking. This scope — targeting the real, documented problems of a specific type of agricultural retailer rather than delivering a generic feature set — offers the sector a practical template. Businesses considering their own procurement modernization efforts will find in this study both a worked example and an evaluated outcome to calibrate their expectations against.

**1.5 Scope and Delimitation**

This study will focus exclusively on the purchasing and procurement operations of Villon Farm Supply, an agricultural supply store located in a rural community in the Philippines. The scope of the study encompasses the development and evaluation of the Purchasing Module of the Villon Integrated Supply and Trade Application (VISTA), which will address six specific operational areas: vendor directory management, purchase order lifecycle management, goods receiving and delivery verification, accounts payable tracking, price change detection and retail price adjustment, and reorder suggestion generation. These functional areas correspond directly to the six identified problems in the current manual purchasing system and represent the core requirements that the developed module will satisfy.

The participants of this study will be limited to the Manager and Owner of Villon Farm Supply, who are the only individuals who interact with the purchasing system on a daily and strategic basis, respectively. The evaluation of the developed module will be conducted exclusively within the setting of Villon Farm Supply, and the findings will reflect the specific operational context, business practices, and user characteristics of this particular establishment. The system will be developed as a Windows Presentation Foundation (WPF) desktop application using VB.NET 10, with a MariaDB central database for server-side data management and a SQLite offline-first architecture to ensure continued functionality in environments with intermittent network connectivity.

This study will not cover the Inventory Module, the Point-of-Sale Module, or the Accounting Module of VISTA, which are planned as separate modules in the larger VISTA system. The developed Purchasing Module will not be deployed on mobile platforms or through web-based interfaces, as the business currently operates exclusively from a fixed desktop workstation. Integration with external supplier systems or electronic data interchange (EDI) capabilities will not be included, as the suppliers of Villon Farm Supply currently communicate through traditional channels such as phone calls and text messages. Additionally, the study will not attempt to implement machine learning or artificial intelligence algorithms for demand forecasting, although the reorder suggestion engine will employ statistical methods based on historical consumption data and lead times. These delimitations are necessary to maintain a focused scope that can be realistically achieved within the constraints of the study while still addressing the most critical purchasing problems identified at Villon Farm Supply.

**1.6 Definition of Terms**

The following terms relevant in this study must be defined operationally to facilitate better understanding:

**Purchase Order** refers to a formal, digitally generated document within the VISTA Purchasing Module that authorizes the procurement of agricultural supplies from a specific vendor, specifying the items, quantities, agreed-upon prices, and expected delivery dates. In this study, the purchase order serves as the foundational transaction record that initiates the purchasing workflow and establishes an auditable trail from requisition to payment.

**Vendor Directory** refers to a centralized digital registry within the VISTA Purchasing Module that stores and organizes all supplier information, including contact details, product catalogs, pricing agreements, and transaction history. In this study, the vendor directory replaces the informal and fragmented supplier records currently maintained in physical notebooks and provides a single source of truth for all vendor-related data.

**Goods Receiving** refers to the process within the VISTA Purchasing Module of recording, verifying, and accepting deliveries from suppliers against outstanding purchase orders. In this study, goods receiving involves matching delivered items to purchase order specifications, documenting discrepancies, and updating the system to reflect received quantities, thereby closing the loop between order placement and delivery confirmation.

**Accounts Payable** refers to the formal tracking mechanism within the VISTA Purchasing Module that records all financial obligations of Villon Farm Supply to its suppliers arising from purchase transactions. In this study, accounts payable replaces the informal credit ledger currently used and provides structured records of amounts owed, payment due dates, and payment histories to ensure that all supplier obligations are accurately monitored and fulfilled.

**Reorder Point** refers to the calculated inventory threshold within the VISTA Purchasing Module at which a new purchase order should be generated to replenish stock before it is depleted. In this study, the reorder point is determined programmatically based on historical consumption rates and supplier lead times, replacing the subjective, gut-feel approach currently employed by Villon Farm Supply.

**Lead Time** refers to the elapsed time between the placement of a purchase order and the receipt of the ordered goods, as tracked within the VISTA Purchasing Module. In this study, lead time is a critical variable used in the calculation of reorder points and safety stock levels, and its accurate recording enables the system to generate timely procurement recommendations.

**FIFO Costing** refers to the First-In, First-Out cost accounting method that the VISTA Purchasing Module will employ to assign costs to goods sold, wherein the earliest purchased items are assumed to be sold first. In this study, FIFO costing provides a systematic approach to calculating the cost of goods sold that reflects the actual flow of agricultural inventory and improves the accuracy of financial reporting.

**Price Volatility** refers to the frequent and often unpredictable fluctuations in the purchase prices of agricultural supplies, particularly fertilizers and animal feeds, that result from global commodity market dynamics and currency exchange rate movements. In this study, price volatility is addressed through the price change detection feature of the VISTA Purchasing Module, which automatically flags supplier price changes and prompts corresponding updates to retail pricing.

**Purchase Order Lifecycle** refers to the complete sequence of states that a purchase order traverses within the VISTA Purchasing Module, from creation and approval, through dispatch to the supplier, goods receiving, and finally to closure upon full delivery and payment. In this study, the purchase order lifecycle provides the structural framework for audit trails and accountability, ensuring that every procurement transaction is tracked from initiation to completion.

**MerchSys.Purchasing** refers to the specific namespace and module designation within the VISTA application architecture that encapsulates all purchasing-related functionality, including vendor management, purchase order processing, goods receiving, accounts payable tracking, price change detection, and reorder suggestion generation. In this study, MerchSys.Purchasing represents the complete software component that will be developed, evaluated against ISO 25010:2023 standards, and assessed for user acceptability using the UTAUT2 framework.

***Purchasing and Procurement Systems in Agricultural Supply***

Agricultural supply businesses occupy a unique position in the broader supply chain, serving as the critical intermediary between manufacturers of farming inputs and the end-user farmers who depend on timely access to fertilizers, pesticides, seeds, and animal feeds. The purchasing and procurement functions within these businesses are inherently more complex than those in many other retail sectors due to the combined effects of seasonality, perishability of certain inputs, and extreme price volatility driven by global commodity markets. According to the Collaborative Planning, Forecasting, and Replenishment (CPFR) model empirically evaluated by Hill et al. (2018), the integration of purchasing planning with supplier collaboration and demand forecasting can significantly reduce procurement costs and improve supply chain responsiveness in supply chain contexts. The CPFR approach emphasizes that purchasing decisions should not be made in isolation but rather as part of a collaborative process that involves information sharing between buyers and suppliers, joint business planning, and synchronized order generation.

The challenges specific to agricultural supply chains have been well documented in the literature. Seasonality creates pronounced demand peaks during planting and harvest seasons, requiring agricultural supply businesses to ramp up procurement activities during predictable periods while maintaining leaner inventories during off-season months (Yadav et al., 2022). Perishability concerns, while more directly relevant to fresh produce, also affect certain agricultural inputs such as biological fertilizers and treated seeds that have limited shelf lives and must be procured and sold within specific time windows. Price volatility represents perhaps the most significant challenge, as the prices of key agricultural inputs such as fertilizers are directly tied to global energy markets and can fluctuate dramatically within short periods. For small agricultural supply businesses that operate on thin margins and lack the purchasing power to negotiate fixed-price contracts, these price fluctuations can erode profitability and create significant challenges in maintaining consistent retail pricing (Yang et al., 2022).

In the Philippine context, the challenges of agricultural procurement are amplified by the fragmented nature of the supply chain and the limited digital infrastructure available to small and medium enterprises. Many agricultural supply stores in rural areas continue to rely on traditional procurement methods, including verbal orders placed through phone calls or text messages, handwritten purchase records, and manual tracking of supplier obligations. These practices not only increase the risk of errors and miscommunication but also make it extremely difficult to maintain the audit trails and documentation that are increasingly required for regulatory compliance, particularly for businesses registered with the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). The experience of Villon Farm Supply reflects these broader industry challenges, as the business currently operates with a fully manual purchasing system that exhibits all of the problems identified in the literature, including the absence of data-driven reorder decisions, lack of formal purchase order documentation, and inadequate tracking of accounts payable obligations.

The CPFR model provides a theoretical foundation for addressing these challenges, as it advocates for a shift from reactive, order-driven purchasing to proactive, collaboration-driven procurement planning. However, the full implementation of CPFR requires significant technological infrastructure and organizational maturity that may not be feasible for small agricultural supply businesses. A more pragmatic approach, as supported by the literature, involves the incremental adoption of digital tools that address the most critical pain points in the purchasing process, starting with the formalization of purchase orders and the establishment of data-driven reorder mechanisms (Alabdali & Salam, 2022). This incremental approach aligns with the developmental strategy of the VISTA Purchasing Module, which prioritizes the automation of core purchasing functions while laying the groundwork for more advanced collaborative features in future iterations.

***Digital Transformation of Manual Business Processes***

The digital transformation of manual business processes has been a subject of extensive research and practice over the past two decades, driven by the increasing availability and affordability of information technology solutions. Digital transformation, in the context of small and medium enterprises, refers to the integration of digital technologies into core business processes to fundamentally change how the business operates and delivers value to its customers (Verhoef et al., 2021). In the domain of purchasing and procurement, digital transformation typically involves the replacement of manual, paper-based processes with digital systems that automate transaction recording, facilitate information flow, and enable data-driven decision-making. The benefits of such transformation have been well documented in the literature and include improved accuracy and completeness of records, enhanced visibility into purchasing activities, reduced processing times, and stronger internal controls through automated audit trails.

The impact of digital transformation on inventory management and purchasing operations has been examined in a comprehensive study by Rane et al. (2024), who found that the adoption of digital tools for procurement and inventory management resulted in significant improvements in order accuracy, supplier communication efficiency, and cost reduction across a sample of small and medium enterprises. Their findings indicate that businesses that transitioned from manual to digital purchasing systems experienced an average reduction of 23 percent in procurement cycle times and a 31 percent improvement in order accuracy. These improvements were attributed primarily to the elimination of manual data entry errors, the standardization of purchase order formats, and the automation of approval workflows that reduced bottlenecks and delays in the procurement process. The study further noted that the availability of digital records enabled more effective analysis of purchasing patterns, which in turn supported better negotiation with suppliers and more strategic procurement planning.

Despite the documented benefits, the digital transformation of small business purchasing processes faces several significant barriers that must be acknowledged and addressed. Cost remains a primary concern, as many small businesses lack the capital to invest in enterprise-grade procurement software and the associated infrastructure upgrades (Mikalef et al., 2020). Technical complexity represents another barrier, as the implementation and maintenance of digital purchasing systems often require information technology expertise that is not available in-house at small businesses. User resistance is also a well-documented challenge, particularly in businesses where long-standing manual processes are deeply embedded in organizational culture and where staff may lack confidence in their ability to use digital tools effectively. The UTAUT2 model developed by Venkatesh et al. (2012) provides a useful framework for understanding and addressing these adoption barriers, as it identifies the key factors that influence user acceptance of technology, including performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, hedonic motivation, price value, and habit.

The development of the EzStock inventory management system for small businesses, as documented by Lim et al. (2025), provides a relevant case study for understanding how digital transformation can be successfully implemented in resource-constrained environments. The EzStock system was designed specifically for small retail businesses that lacked the technical infrastructure and expertise to adopt conventional enterprise resource planning solutions. The study found that a user-centered design approach, combined with a focus on core functionality and simplicity, resulted in high levels of user acceptance even among respondents with limited computer literacy. This finding is particularly relevant to the present study, as Villon Farm Supply operates in a similar context where the end users have limited experience with digital purchasing systems. The lesson from the EzStock experience is that digital transformation for small businesses must prioritize ease of use and must address the most pressing operational pain points first, rather than attempting to replicate the full feature set of enterprise solutions. This principle has guided the design of the VISTA Purchasing Module, which focuses on automating the six most critical purchasing problems identified at Villon Farm Supply while maintaining a simple and intuitive user interface.

***Reorder Management and Inventory Forecasting***

Reorder management and inventory forecasting represent critical components of effective purchasing operations, as they determine when and how much inventory should be procured to meet anticipated demand while minimizing the costs associated with overstocking and stockouts. The fundamental concept underlying reorder management is the reorder point, which represents the inventory level at which a new purchase order should be placed to replenish stock before it is depleted. The classical reorder point model, as described by Silver et al. (2017), is expressed as the product of average daily demand and lead time, plus a safety stock component that provides a buffer against demand variability and supply uncertainty. In formulaic terms, the reorder point equals the expected demand during lead time plus the safety stock, where safety stock is determined by the desired service level, the standard deviation of demand during lead time, and the Z-score corresponding to the service level target.

The application of reorder point models in agricultural supply contexts presents unique challenges that are not fully addressed by classical inventory theory. Agricultural supply businesses face seasonal demand patterns that deviate significantly from the stationary demand assumptions underlying standard reorder point calculations. During planting seasons, demand for fertilizers and seeds can increase by several hundred percent relative to off-season periods, creating demand spikes that can overwhelm static reorder point calculations (Yadav et al., 2022). Conversely, the demand for certain inputs may drop to near-zero during off-season periods, leading to excessive inventory carrying costs if reorder points are not adjusted accordingly. These seasonal patterns necessitate the use of time-varying reorder point models that incorporate seasonal indices or the use of separate reorder parameters for different seasons. The literature on seasonal demand forecasting in agricultural supply chains suggests that even simple seasonal adjustment methods, such as the use of multiplicative seasonal indices applied to historical average demand, can significantly improve the accuracy of reorder decisions compared to static approaches that ignore seasonality.

The superiority of data-driven reorder decisions over gut-feel approaches has been consistently demonstrated in the inventory management literature. A foundational study by the University of Minnesota Agricultural Economics department on farmers' supply purchasing practices found that businesses relying on intuition and experience for reorder decisions experienced significantly higher rates of both stockouts and overstocking compared to those using systematic reorder point calculations. The study attributed this difference to the inherent limitations of human judgment in processing the multiple variables that influence optimal reorder decisions, including demand rates, lead times, demand variability, and cost parameters. More recent studies have confirmed these findings, with Syntetos et al. (2016) demonstrating that even relatively simple statistical forecasting methods outperform judgmental forecasts in inventory management contexts, particularly when demand patterns exhibit trends or seasonality.

For the VISTA Purchasing Module, the reorder suggestion engine will incorporate the core principles of data-driven reorder management while accounting for the specific characteristics of agricultural supply inventory. The system will calculate reorder points based on historical consumption data and recorded lead times, with adjustments for seasonal demand patterns based on the analysis of historical sales data across multiple periods. The reorder suggestion engine will not employ complex machine learning algorithms, as the volume of historical data available at Villon Farm Supply (approximately 50 SKUs with limited transaction history) may not be sufficient to train such models reliably. Instead, the system will use established statistical methods, including moving average demand calculations and lead time analysis, to generate reorder recommendations that represent a significant improvement over the current gut-feel approach. The literature supports this pragmatic approach, as several studies have found that the incremental benefit of sophisticated forecasting methods diminishes rapidly when data availability is limited and when the primary objective is to establish a baseline level of systematic reorder management rather than to achieve forecasting optimality.

***Software Quality Evaluation Using ISO 25010***

The evaluation of software quality is a critical component of any systems development study, as it provides an objective and structured basis for determining whether the developed system meets the requirements and expectations of its intended users. The ISO/IEC 25010 standard, published by the International Organization for Standardization, provides a comprehensive framework for the evaluation of software product quality that has been widely adopted in both academic research and industry practice. The standard defines eight quality characteristics that collectively represent the properties of a software product that determine its ability to satisfy stated and implied needs: functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability (ISO, 2023). Each of these characteristics is further decomposed into sub-characteristics that provide more granular criteria for evaluation.

Functional suitability refers to the degree to which a software product provides functions that meet stated and implied needs when used under specified conditions. This characteristic encompasses three sub-characteristics: functional completeness, which assesses whether the software covers all specified tasks and user objectives; functional correctness, which evaluates whether the software produces accurate and consistent results; and functional appropriateness, which determines whether the software facilitates the accomplishment of specified tasks. In the context of the VISTA Purchasing Module, functional suitability will be evaluated by assessing whether the module implements all of the required purchasing functions, whether those functions produce correct results, and whether the implemented functions are appropriate for the purchasing operations of Villon Farm Supply.

Performance efficiency refers to the performance relative to the amount of resources used under stated conditions, and it includes sub-characteristics of time behavior, resource utilization, and capacity. Compatibility addresses the ability of the software to exchange information with other systems and to operate in different environments, encompassing co-existence and interoperability. Usability evaluates the degree to which a software product can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals with effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction in a specified context of use, including appropriateness recognizability, learnability, operability, user error protection, user interface aesthetics, and accessibility. Reliability measures the degree to which a system or component performs specified functions under specified conditions for a specified period, covering maturity, availability, fault tolerance, and recoverability. Security assesses the degree to which a product protects information and data so that persons or other products have the degree of data access appropriate to their types and levels of authorization, including confidentiality, integrity, non-repudiation, accountability, and authenticity. Maintainability evaluates the effectiveness and efficiency with which a product can be modified, including modularity, reusability, analysability, modifiability, and testability. Portability measures the ease with which a product can be transferred from one environment to another, covering adaptability, installability, and replaceability.

The application of ISO 25010 to the evaluation of information systems has been demonstrated in numerous studies across various domains. Susanto and Al-Obaidi (2023) applied the ISO 25010 quality model to evaluate a web-based academic information system and found that the framework provided a comprehensive and structured approach to quality assessment that identified both strengths and areas for improvement across multiple quality dimensions. Their study highlighted the importance of using a standardized quality framework rather than ad hoc evaluation criteria, as the former enables meaningful comparison across different systems and contexts. Similarly, the use of ISO 25010 in the evaluation of the VISTA Purchasing Module will provide a rigorous and comparable assessment of the developed system's quality, ensuring that the evaluation results are not only relevant to the specific context of Villon Farm Supply but also comparable to evaluations of similar systems in the broader literature. The 2023 revision of the ISO 25010 standard, which updated certain sub-characteristics and clarified definitions, will be used as the basis for the evaluation instrument in this study.

**METHODOLOGY**

**3.1 Research Design**

This study will follow a descriptive-developmental research design. The pairing of descriptive and developmental methods is appropriate here because the study’s four statements of the problem require two fundamentally different modes of inquiry to address. SOP #1 requires that the researchers document what is actually happening in the purchasing operations of Villon Farm Supply right now — what the manager does when stock runs low, how supplier orders are placed and recorded, how price changes are handled, and how accounts payable is tracked. This is a descriptive task: it requires direct observation and structured interviews rather than assumptions, and it produces an evidence-based account of six specific problems (P1 through P6) that the system must resolve. Descriptive research is suited to this purpose because it captures the existing state of a phenomenon without manipulating it, providing the empirical foundation that development work requires (Creswell & Creswell, 2018).

The developmental component of the research design will address the second statement of the problem (SOP #2), which seeks to determine the specific requirements and interventions needed to provide solutions to the current practices, issues, and problems encountered in the purchasing operations. This component follows the systems development life cycle (SDLC) methodology, which provides a structured framework for translating user requirements into a functional information system. The SDLC approach involves systematic phases of requirements analysis, system design, implementation, testing, and deployment, each of which produces documented outputs that ensure traceability from identified problems to implemented solutions. The developmental component will result in the creation of the VISTA Purchasing Module, which will be designed to address the six identified purchasing problems through the implementation of a vendor directory, purchase order lifecycle management, goods receiving, accounts payable tracking, price change detection, and a reorder suggestion engine.

SOP #3 and SOP #4 require a shift from building to assessing. SOP #3 evaluates the completed purchasing module against the eight quality characteristics of ISO 25010:2023 — an activity that is descriptive in method (systematic measurement against defined criteria) but only possible after the developmental phase has produced something to evaluate. SOP #4 assesses whether the Manager and Owner actually find the module acceptable and usable, measured through the UTAUT2 framework. The descriptive-developmental design is the only design that unifies all four SOPs within a coherent methodology — one where problem identification, solution development, technical evaluation, and user acceptance assessment are treated not as separate studies but as sequential phases of a single integrated research effort. The integrity of each phase depends on what the previous phase produced: the features built in SOP #2 are only valid if they trace back to findings in SOP #1, and the evaluation results in SOP #3 and SOP #4 are only meaningful because they assess a system built from those findings.

**3.2 Locale of the Study**

This study will be conducted at Villon Farm Supply, an agricultural supply and trade business in a rural farming community in the Philippines. From a purchasing perspective, the store operates at the end of a supply chain that is subject to the same price volatility, seasonal demand cycles, and supplier relationship dynamics that characterize agricultural input markets across the region. The store sources fertilizers, pesticides, seeds, and animal feeds from multiple suppliers, managing approximately 50 distinct SKUs whose costs fluctuate between orders in response to global commodity prices and local supply conditions. Every new purchase requires the manager to call a supplier, negotiate or confirm the current price, and then decide whether to order — without any system to compare that price against the last recorded purchase cost, flag a margin impact, or suggest an order quantity based on how fast the product has been selling. The entire purchasing function, from need recognition to goods receipt to AP tracking, runs through a single physical record book.

Villon Farm Supply was selected for this study because it presents all six purchasing problems the module is designed to address, and because the agricultural input context — with its seasonal demand spikes during palay planting and growing periods, its supplier price volatility, and its multi-supplier product sourcing — provides the full range of scenarios the reorder suggestion engine and price change detection features need to handle. A store selling only non-seasonal, price-stable goods would not adequately test the seasonal demand flagging or price volatility response features that are central to this module’s design. The business is also BIR-registered and has been audited, which means the audit trail problem (P2) is not hypothetical — there is a real regulatory context in which the absence of formal purchasing records carries consequences. This combination of operational relevance and regulatory context makes Villon Farm Supply a well-grounded locale for this study.

**3.3 Respondents and Sampling Techniques**

The respondents for this study will be the Manager and Owner of Villon Farm Supply, selected through purposive sampling (also referred to as judgmental sampling), a non-probability technique in which participants are chosen based on specific criteria tied to the research objectives (Etikan et al., 2016). For this study, the inclusion criterion is that the respondent must have direct, firsthand involvement in the purchasing operations at Villon Farm Supply — either as the person who executes purchasing activities or as the person responsible for their strategic and financial oversight. The Manager satisfies this criterion operationally: it is the Manager who calls suppliers to place orders, walks the shelves to estimate what needs replenishing, updates the price book when a supplier quotes a new cost, and receives and records deliveries in the physical record book. Every one of the six identified problems (P1 through P6) is encountered directly in the Manager’s daily work. The Owner satisfies the criterion strategically: purchasing expenditures are the primary driver of the business’s cost structure, and the Owner bears the financial consequences of over-ordering, under-ordering, missed AP due dates, and undetected margin erosion from price changes.

Purposive sampling suits this study because firsthand involvement in supplier transactions is a prerequisite for meaningful evaluation. The Manager is the person who initiates purchase orders, coordinates delivery receipts, and flags price discrepancies on a daily basis — making their experience with problems P1 through P6 direct and traceable rather than secondhand. The Owner, for their part, bears the financial consequences when procurement costs drift or when a supplier relationship sours, giving them a stake in the strategic dimensions the module is designed to address. No other member of the organization occupies either of these roles simultaneously, so broadening the sample beyond these two individuals would not add evaluative depth — it would dilute it. A two-respondent developmental study of this kind prioritizes depth of evaluation over statistical breadth, which is consistent with the descriptive-developmental research design adopted here. **3.4 Research Instruments**

This study will use two researcher-made survey questionnaires as the primary instruments for data collection, each corresponding to a specific statement of the problem.

The first instrument is the **ISO 25010 Compliance Evaluation Form**, which corresponds to SOP #3 and is designed to measure the extent of compliance of the developed purchasing module with the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 Software Quality Standards. This instrument is researcher-made and is based on the eight quality characteristics defined in the ISO/IEC 25010 standard, namely functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability. The evaluation form is organized into eight sub-sections, each corresponding to one quality characteristic and containing five to eight items that assess specific aspects of that characteristic. Each item is measured using a five-point Likert scale, where 1 indicates Strongly Disagree and 5 indicates Strongly Agree. The total number of items across all eight sub-sections ranges from 40 to 64, providing a comprehensive assessment of the developed module's quality across all dimensions of the ISO 25010 framework.

The second instrument is the **UTAUT2 Acceptability Survey**, which corresponds to SOP #4 and is designed to measure the level of acceptability and usability of the developed purchasing module from the perspective of end users. This instrument is modified from the original UTAUT2 instrument developed by Venkatesh et al. (2012) and has been adapted for the context of a desktop purchasing application in an agricultural supply business. The survey focuses on two primary constructs as specified in the statement of the problem: User Acceptance and Perceived Ease of Use and Usefulness as Efficacy. Each construct is measured through a set of items using a five-point Likert scale, where 1 indicates Strongly Disagree and 5 indicates Strongly Agree. The adaptation of the original UTAUT2 instrument involves the modification of item wording to reflect the specific features and usage context of the VISTA Purchasing Module, while preserving the theoretical constructs and measurement approach of the original instrument.

Both instruments will be subjected to content validation by experts, including information technology professionals and a panel of advisers, who will review the items for clarity, relevance, and representativeness of the constructs being measured. Revisions will be made based on the feedback received during the content validation process. The reliability of both instruments will be tested using Cronbach's alpha, with a threshold of 0.70 set as the minimum acceptable reliability coefficient. Instruments that achieve a Cronbach's alpha of 0.70 or higher will be considered sufficiently reliable for use in the study, consistent with the accepted standard in social science research (Taber, 2018).

**3.5 Data Gathering Procedure**

The data gathering procedure for this study will follow a systematic, step-by-step process designed to ensure the thoroughness and validity of the data collected. The procedure will proceed as follows.

First, the researcher will seek permission from the owner of Villon Farm Supply to conduct the study within the business premises and to involve the Manager and Owner as respondents. This permission will be obtained through a formal request letter that outlines the purpose, scope, and expected duration of the study, as well as the nature and extent of participation required from the respondents. Second, upon receiving permission, the researcher will conduct an initial interview with the Manager to document the current purchasing practices, issues, and problems experienced at Villon Farm Supply. This interview will address SOP #1 and will be guided by a semi-structured interview protocol that covers the six identified problem areas: reorder decision-making, audit trail documentation, retail price updates, accounts payable tracking, price volatility management, and seasonal demand planning. The interview will be recorded with the respondent's consent and will be transcribed for analysis.

Third, the researcher will analyze the data gathered from the interview to determine the specific requirements and interventions needed to address the identified problems, thereby addressing SOP #2. This analysis will involve thematic analysis of the interview responses, mapping of identified problems to system features, and the development of a requirements specification document that will guide the design and development of the VISTA Purchasing Module. Fourth, the researcher will develop the Purchasing Module of VISTA based on the identified requirements, following the systems development life cycle methodology. The development process will include detailed system design, coding in VB.NET 10 using the WPF framework, unit testing, integration testing, and user acceptance testing.

Fifth, the researcher will present the developed system to the respondents for evaluation. This presentation will include a demonstration of the system's features and functionality, as well as a hands-on session where the respondents will have the opportunity to interact with the system and perform typical purchasing tasks. Sixth, after the respondents have had sufficient exposure to the system, the researcher will administer the ISO 25010 Compliance Evaluation Form and the UTAUT2 Acceptability Survey. The respondents will complete both instruments independently, and the researcher will be available to clarify any questions about the instruments. Seventh, the researcher will collect and organize the responses from both instruments for analysis, ensuring that all data is accurately recorded and properly coded for statistical processing.

**3.6 Data Analysis Plan**

The data analysis plan for this study is organized according to the order of the statements of the problem, with each analysis method directly corresponding to the type of data collected for each research question.

For **SOP #1** (practices, issues, and problems encountered in the existing manual purchasing system), the data gathered from the semi-structured interview will be analyzed using qualitative methods. Thematic analysis will be employed to identify, organize, and interpret the patterns and themes that emerge from the interview responses regarding the current purchasing practices at Villon Farm Supply. This analysis will involve the coding of interview transcripts, the identification of recurring themes and patterns, and the development of thematic categories that capture the range of practices, issues, and problems reported by the respondents. Additionally, frequency counts of the identified problems will be tabulated to provide a quantitative summary of the most commonly cited issues.

For **SOP #2** (requirements and interventions needed to address the identified problems), the analysis will also be qualitative in nature. The requirements specification will be derived from the interview data through a process of requirement elicitation and analysis. This process will involve mapping each identified problem to one or more system features or interventions that can address the problem, producing a traceability matrix that links problems to requirements to implemented solutions. The requirement specification document will serve as both an output of the analysis and an input to the system development process.

For **SOP #3** (compliance of the developed purchasing module with ISO 25010:2023 Software Quality Standards), the data from the ISO 25010 Compliance Evaluation Form will be analyzed using quantitative methods. The weighted mean for each of the eight quality characteristics will be calculated, and each mean score will be interpreted using the following descriptive scale: 4.50 to 5.00 indicates Strongly Agree, 3.50 to 4.49 indicates Agree, 2.50 to 3.49 indicates Undecided, 1.50 to 2.49 indicates Disagree, and 1.00 to 1.49 indicates Strongly Disagree. This analysis will provide a profile of the developed module's quality across all eight dimensions of the ISO 25010 framework, identifying areas of strength and areas that may require further improvement.

For **SOP #4** (level of acceptability and usability of the developed purchasing module using UTAUT2), the data from the UTAUT2 Acceptability Survey will be analyzed using quantitative methods. The weighted mean for each UTAUT2 construct, specifically User Acceptance and Perceived Ease of Use and Usefulness as Efficacy, will be calculated and interpreted using the same five-point descriptive scale employed for SOP #3. This analysis will determine the overall acceptability of the developed module from the perspective of the end users and will identify specific aspects of the system that contribute to or hinder user acceptance.

If inferential statistics are employed, the level of significance will be set at alpha equals 0.05. However, given the small sample size of two respondents, the primary analytical approach will be descriptive, with weighted means and descriptive interpretations forming the basis of the quantitative analysis. The combination of qualitative and quantitative analytical methods ensures that the study addresses all four statements of the problem with appropriate rigor and that the findings provide both descriptive depth and quantitative precision.

**3.7 Ethical Considerations**

The participants will be informed of the purpose of the study, and to ensure ethics was taken into consideration, an informed consent form will be filled out. A consent will also be obtained prior to the interview. To ensure anonymity, all information will be treated with utmost confidentiality in accordance with RA 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012.
