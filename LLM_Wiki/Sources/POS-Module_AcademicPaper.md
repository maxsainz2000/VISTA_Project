**CHAPTER 1**

**INTRODUCTION**

**1.1 Background of the Study**

At its most fundamental level, a point of sale transaction is an act of trust: a customer hands over money or commits a debt, and the business records it accurately, issues proof of the exchange, and stands behind that record if it is ever questioned. In small retail businesses that still process every sale manually — through handwritten receipt books, informal ledger entries, and cash counts that rely on memory — that record is fragile. It can be lost, disputed, or simply never made at all. The global shift toward digital POS systems over the past two decades has been driven precisely by this fragility: the recognition that transaction accountability, receipt compliance, and credit management cannot be sustained at scale on paper. The global POS software market, valued at approximately USD 10.07 billion in 2022 and growing at a compound annual rate of 10.8% through 2030 (Grand View Research, 2023), reflects how widely this recognition has spread among businesses in developed markets. What it does not yet reflect is how many small businesses in developing economies are still waiting for practical, affordable solutions that fit their specific context.

In the Philippines, that gap is particularly visible in the agricultural retail sector. The DTI (2023) reports that MSMEs account for approximately 99.5% of all registered businesses in the country, yet a large share of them — especially those in rural and semi-urban areas — continue to issue handwritten receipts and track credit through informal ledgers. The Bureau of Internal Revenue requires all registered businesses to issue official receipts for every transaction, but many small stores lack the systems to do so consistently. This is compounded by the practice of utang — the informal extension of credit to customers who settle their balances after harvest — which is culturally embedded in agricultural communities. While utang sustains commerce and builds long-term customer relationships, managing it through handwritten notes and memory inevitably produces disputes, lost records, and uncollected receivables that quietly erode the business’s financial position.

In the province of Sultan Kudarat, and more specifically in the municipality of Tacurong City, agricultural retail businesses face unique operational challenges that are exacerbated by their reliance on manual sales processes. Villon Farm Supply, a locally operated agricultural retail store, currently records all sales transactions manually using a receipt book, maintains credit accounts through informal ledgers, and lacks an automated official receipt numbering system for Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) compliance. The store processes multiple payment types including cash, GCash, bank transfer, and credit (utang), yet there is no unified digital system to manage these diverse payment modes. The absence of a formal POS system has resulted in several identified problems: sales recorded manually in a receipt book with no digital record or searchable history (S1), credit or utang tracked informally with no formal ledger leading to balances easily lost or disputed (S2), no official receipt number system for BIR compliance (S3), no mechanism to block repeat credit for customers with unpaid balances (S4), and no automated daily sales summary (S5).

The VISTA POS Module is the proposed solution to these five problems. It will replace the receipt book with a cart-based digital transaction interface that supports all four payment modes the store currently accepts — cash, GCash, bank transfer, and credit — and automatically generates official receipts in the BIR-required OR-YYYY-XXXX format for every completed sale. Credit customers will have formal digital accounts with running balance tracking, and the system will enforce a hard block on additional credit purchases for any customer with an outstanding balance. At the end of each day, the module will produce an automated sales summary that breaks down total revenue by payment method and transaction count, replacing the informal mental tallying the manager currently does at closing time.

The contribution of this study goes beyond solving the immediate transactional problems at one store. The combination of challenges at Villon Farm Supply — informal utang management, BIR receipt compliance, multi-payment-mode support, and the absence of daily sales visibility — is shared by a large number of agricultural retail MSMEs across the Philippines. A POS module that addresses all four of these requirements within a single offline-capable desktop application, evaluated against ISO 25010:2023 and UTAUT2, provides the sector with a tested design reference rather than a hypothetical recommendation. The findings of this study will be of direct use to other developers building similar systems and to researchers studying technology adoption in Philippine agricultural retail contexts.

**1.2 Conceptual Framework**

The conceptual framework of this study maps the relationship between the five identified sales transaction problems at Villon Farm Supply and the specific digital capabilities the VISTA POS Module will introduce to resolve them. The framework is organized using the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model, which makes explicit how the current state of sales operations will be transformed through the development process into a new operational state. The independent variables are the conditions that define the current problem: the manual receipt book, the informal utang ledger, the missing BIR receipt numbering system, the absence of credit blocking, and the lack of any automated daily sales summary. The dependent variables are the outcomes the module will produce: digital transaction records, automated BIR-compliant receipts, enforced credit policies, and real-time sales reporting. The development and implementation of the VISTA POS Module itself serves as the intervening variable that connects these two states.

**Input.** The input component of the framework encompasses the current manual sales practices employed at Villon Farm Supply, which include handwritten receipt books, informal credit ledgers, and the absence of an official receipt numbering system. Additionally, the input includes the five identified problems (S1 through S5) that characterize the existing operational deficiencies: the lack of digital sales records, the informality of credit tracking, the absence of BIR-compliant receipt numbering, the inability to block credit for customers with unpaid balances, and the lack of automated daily sales summaries. The business requirements for transaction processing, including support for multiple payment modes (cash, GCash, bank transfer, and credit), discount management, sales return and exchange handling, and VAT-ready transaction structures, also form part of the input variables that will inform the system design.

**Process.** The process component involves the development and implementation of the VISTA POS Module, which will transform the identified inputs into functional outputs. The development process will encompass the design and coding of the transaction processing interface with a cart-based layout, the implementation of payment mode support for cash, GCash, bank transfer, and credit, the creation of an automated official receipt generation system following the OR-YYYY-XXXX format, the development of credit customer management functionality with blocking logic for unpaid accounts, the integration of discount management and sales return capabilities, the establishment of a VAT-ready transaction structure, the implementation of daily sales summary reporting, and the inclusion of accounts receivable collection flags for credit accounts.

**Output.** The output component represents the tangible results of the development process: a fully functional digital POS system with auditable transaction records, automated official receipt generation ensuring BIR compliance, enforced credit policies that prevent customers with unpaid balances from making additional credit purchases, real-time daily sales summaries that provide business owners and managers with immediate visibility into store performance, and a comprehensive transaction history with search and filter capabilities. The output will also include credit account management with payment recording, customer status tracking, and accounts receivable visibility.

**1.3 Statement of the Problem**

This study aims to develop and evaluate the Point of Sale (POS) Module of the Villon Integrated Supply and Trade Application (VISTA) for Villon Farm Supply. Specifically, this study will seek to answer the following questions:

> 1. What are the **practices, issues, and problems** encountered by Villon Farm Supply in using their existing manual point of sale system?
>
> 2. What specific **requirements and interventions** are needed to provide solutions to the current practices, issues, and problems encountered in sales transactions?
>
> 3. What is the extent of **compliance of the developed POS module** based on **ISO 25010:2023** in terms of: (a) Functional Suitability, (b) Performance Efficiency, (c) Compatibility, (d) Usability, (e) Reliability, (f) Security, (g) Maintainability, and (h) Portability?
>
> 4. What is the **level of acceptability and usability** of the developed POS module using **UTAUT2** in terms of: (a) User Acceptance; and (b) Perceived Ease of Use and Usefulness as Efficacy?

**1.4 Significance of the Study**

**Business Owners.** The business owners of Villon Farm Supply will gain something that the current manual system cannot provide: a verifiable record of every transaction the store has ever processed. At present, if a credit balance is disputed or a receipt is questioned, there is no authoritative digital record to reference. The VISTA POS Module will change that. Every sale — regardless of payment method — will be logged with a timestamp, items, amounts, and a sequential receipt number. Owners will be able to review the day’s transactions, the week’s credit activity, and the outstanding AR balance from a single screen without having to ask the manager for a hand-tallied summary.

**Store Managers.** Store managers will benefit most directly from the day-to-day operational improvements the POS Module introduces. Currently, the manager must write receipts by hand, calculate change manually, and decide on the spot whether to extend further credit to a customer who may already have an unpaid balance — a judgment call made with no reference to any formal record. The VISTA POS Module will replace all of this with a cart-based interface that calculates totals, generates receipts automatically, and enforces credit blocking by system rule rather than managerial discretion. The end-of-day summary, which the manager currently assembles from memory, will be produced automatically. The net result is a reduction in the cognitive and administrative load the manager carries on every transaction.

**Credit Customers.** Credit customers — the farmers and agricultural workers who purchase on utang and settle after harvest — will benefit from having their balances formally tracked. Under the current informal system, a customer’s outstanding balance is whatever the ledger says, and if the ledger entry is missing or illegible, the dispute has no neutral resolution. The VISTA POS Module will maintain a digital credit account for each customer that records every purchase, every payment, and the running balance. When a customer comes in to settle a balance, both parties will be looking at the same auditable record. This transparency protects both the customer and the store from the misunderstandings and relationship damage that untracked credit inevitably produces over time.

**BIR Compliance Officers.** BIR compliance and regulatory oversight will benefit from this study’s demonstration that a small agricultural retail store can be brought into full receipt compliance through an affordable, purpose-built system. The VISTA POS Module will issue a sequentially numbered official receipt in the OR-YYYY-XXXX format for every completed transaction and will retain a complete digital record of all receipts, searchable by date and transaction number. This addresses one of the most persistent compliance gaps among small MSMEs — not the refusal to comply, but the absence of a workable system for doing so. The study provides a concrete example of how the compliance barrier can be lowered through thoughtful software design.

**Future Researchers.** Future researchers will find this study useful as a documented case of POS system design for a context that is underrepresented in the literature: agricultural retail in a Philippine semi-urban setting where utang is a primary transaction mode and BIR receipt compliance is structurally difficult to achieve manually. The evaluation methodology — ISO 25010:2023 paired with UTAUT2 applied to a two-respondent purposive sample in a single-business setting — will provide a replicable approach for similarly scoped developmental studies. The specific findings on compliance ratings and user acceptance scores will serve as baseline data for future comparisons as similar systems are developed and evaluated in related contexts.

**MSMEs in Agricultural Retail.** MSMEs in the agricultural retail sector across the Philippines will benefit from this study as a proof of concept that the specific combination of problems they face — informal credit, multi-payment-mode transactions, BIR receipt requirements, and no digital sales history — can be addressed within a single integrated system that runs on standard desktop hardware without internet dependency. Many agricultural retailers have not adopted digital POS solutions because available commercial options are either too expensive, too generic, or require connectivity they do not have. This study demonstrates that a targeted, context-aware solution is both feasible and evaluable, and provides a starting reference for retailers and developers working toward that goal.

**1.5 Scope and Delimitation**

This study will focus exclusively on the Point of Sale (POS) Module of the Villon Integrated Supply and Trade Application (VISTA). The scope of the POS Module encompasses all customer-facing sales operations, including transaction processing through a cart-based interface, payment mode support for cash, GCash, bank transfer, and credit (utang), credit account management with blocking logic for customers with unpaid balances, official receipt generation following the OR-YYYY-XXXX format, transaction history with search and filter capabilities, and daily sales summary reporting. The system will support discount management, sales return and exchange processing, a VAT-ready transaction structure, and accounts receivable collection flags for credit accounts.

The participants of this study will be limited to the Manager and Owner of Villon Farm Supply, who will serve as the primary respondents for both the requirements gathering and the system evaluation phases. The study will be conducted at Villon Farm Supply, located in Tacurong City, Sultan Kudarat, Philippines. The Manager will have full access to all POS features, including the ability to process new sales, view transaction history, and manage credit accounts, while the Owner will have read-only access limited to transaction history and credit account viewing for financial oversight purposes.

This study will not cover other modules of the VISTA system, such as the Purchasing Module, Inventory Module, and Accounting Module, although the POS Module will interface with the Inventory Module for automatic stock deduction upon sale completion. The study will not include online or e-commerce functionality, mobile POS applications, or integration with external payment gateways beyond the recording of GCash and bank transfer payments. The system will not process actual electronic fund transfers but will merely record these payment modes for transaction documentation purposes. Furthermore, the credit blocking rule will be strictly enforced: customers with any unpaid balance greater than zero will be unable to make additional credit purchases until their existing balance is cleared through a recorded payment. The evaluation of the system will be limited to the ISO 25010:2023 software quality characteristics and the UTAUT2 technology acceptance framework and will not extend to other evaluation methodologies or frameworks.

**1.6 Definition of Terms**

**Point of Sale.** The location and process where a retail transaction is completed, encompassing the hardware and software systems used to process sales, accept payments, and generate receipts. In this study, the point of sale refers specifically to the digital system that will replace the manual receipt book at Villon Farm Supply.

**Official Receipt.** A BIR-mandated document issued by registered businesses to document every sales transaction. In the VISTA POS Module, official receipts will be automatically generated with sequential numbers following the OR-YYYY-XXXX format, containing complete transaction details including item descriptions, quantities, prices, payment method, and totals.

**Credit Account (Utang).** A customer account that tracks purchases made on credit, where the customer receives goods immediately but pays at a later date. In the Philippine agricultural retail context, utang is a common practice where farmers purchase agricultural inputs on credit and settle their balances after harvest. The VISTA POS Module will maintain formal digital credit accounts with outstanding balance tracking.

**Credit Blocking.** A system-enforced rule that prevents customers with existing unpaid credit balances from making additional credit purchases. When a customer's outstanding balance is greater than zero, the POS Module will display a warning and disable the sale completion button, thereby enforcing a zero-tolerance policy on stacking unpaid credit.

**Payment Method.** The mode through which a customer settles a transaction. The VISTA POS Module will support four payment methods: cash (with automatic change calculation), GCash (e-wallet payment), bank transfer (bank-to-bank payment), and credit or utang (charged to a customer credit account).

**Sales Return.** A transaction in which a previously sold item is returned by the customer, resulting in a refund or exchange. The VISTA POS Module will include functionality for processing sales returns and exchanges, adjusting transaction records and inventory accordingly.

**VAT-Ready.** A designation indicating that the system's transaction structure is designed to accommodate Value Added Tax (VAT) calculations and reporting. The VISTA POS Module will implement a VAT-ready structure that will allow for the application of VAT rates to taxable items and the generation of VAT-inclusive or VAT-exclusive totals as required by BIR regulations.

**Daily Sales Summary.** An automated report generated at the end of each business day that aggregates all transactions processed through the POS Module, including total sales, breakdown by payment method, number of transactions, and credit account activity. This feature will replace the manual end-of-day cash counting and reconciliation process.

**Accounts Receivable.** The total amount owed to the business by customers who have purchased on credit. In the VISTA POS Module, accounts receivable will be tracked through the credit accounts management feature, which will display each customer's outstanding balance, payment history, and collection status.

**MerchSys.POS.** The technical designation for the Point of Sale Module within the MerchSys (Merchandise System) architecture of VISTA. This term refers to the software component that handles all transaction processing, receipt generation, and credit management functions within the broader VISTA application.

**CHAPTER 2**

**REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES**

This chapter reviews literature and studies that establish the theoretical and empirical foundation for the development and evaluation of the VISTA POS Module. The reviewed works are organized into four themes that correspond directly to the core design challenges and evaluation frameworks of this study: (a) Point of Sale Systems for Small Businesses; (b) Credit Management and Receivables Tracking; (c) BIR Compliance and Official Receipt Generation; and (d) Software Quality and Technology Acceptance Models. Each theme synthesizes findings from prior research and identifies the gaps or considerations that inform specific decisions in the design of the proposed system.

**Point of Sale Systems for Small Businesses**

The influence of POS systems on business performance has been extensively documented in the literature. Okofu et al. (2025) developed an enhanced point-of-sale system for retail businesses in developing countries and demonstrated that POS systems ensure transactions are more reliable, accurate, and faster compared to manual methods, with automated record-keeping directly reducing operational errors and improving the quality of sales data available to business owners. Their development study affirmed that the transition from manual to digital sales recording represents a fundamental shift in how small businesses manage their daily operations, enabling owners to move from reactive problem-solving to proactive business management based on data-driven insights.

In a related study, Boado et al. (2025) investigated POS system adoption among coffee shop owners in Baliwag City and identified several key factors that influence the successful implementation of POS technology in small food and beverage establishments. The study found that ease of use, system reliability, and the availability of training and support were the most critical determinants of adoption success. The findings also highlighted the importance of designing POS interfaces that accommodate the specific workflow patterns of different business types, noting that generic POS solutions often fail to address the unique operational requirements of specialized retail environments.

Within the Philippine context, Bauzon (2025) conducted a study on the challenges and perceptions of POS adoption among micro and small enterprises in Tacurong City and revealed that while business owners recognized the potential benefits of digital POS systems, several barriers impeded adoption, including the high cost of commercial POS software, limited technical literacy among store operators, and the perception that existing manual processes were sufficient for their current business volume. The study recommended the development of affordable, user-friendly POS solutions specifically designed for the Philippine MSME market, with features that address local business practices such as informal credit arrangements and multi-payment-mode support.

Complementing these findings, a study presented at the ACM conference on cloud-based POS systems for MSMEs, conducted in Sta. Rosa Laguna, Philippines, explored the feasibility of deploying cloud-hosted POS solutions among small retailers in semi-urban settings. The study demonstrated that cloud-based POS systems could overcome the infrastructure limitations of traditional on-premise solutions by reducing upfront hardware costs and providing remote access to sales data. However, the researchers noted that internet connectivity remains a significant challenge in many Philippine localities, suggesting that hybrid solutions with offline capabilities may be more appropriate for businesses in areas with unreliable internet access. These findings directly inform the design of the VISTA POS Module, which will operate as a locally deployed application with local data storage to ensure continuous availability regardless of internet connectivity status.

**Credit Management and Receivables Tracking**

The practice of extending credit to customers, known locally as utang, is deeply embedded in the Philippine retail ecosystem, particularly in agricultural supply stores where farmers frequently purchase inputs on credit against future harvest income. Research by Llanto and Badiola (2022) documented the prevalence of informal credit arrangements among Philippine small businesses, noting that an estimated 60 to 70 percent of agricultural retail transactions in rural areas involve some form of credit extension. The study found that while informal credit facilitates commerce and builds customer loyalty, the absence of formal tracking mechanisms frequently leads to disputes over outstanding balances, lost payment records, and accumulated bad debts that can threaten the financial viability of small retailers.

The challenges of accounts receivable (AR) management for small retailers have been examined by multiple researchers. A study by Garcia and Santos (2023) on AR management practices among Philippine MSMEs found that businesses relying on manual credit ledgers experienced an average of 15 to 20 percent discrepancy between recorded and actual outstanding balances, primarily due to incomplete recording, lost entries, and the absence of systematic payment reconciliation processes. The researchers recommended the adoption of digital credit tracking systems that automatically calculate outstanding balances, record payments against specific invoices, and generate aging reports to help business owners identify overdue accounts and assess credit risk.

Credit blocking and risk mitigation strategies have been explored in the context of enterprise resource planning systems but remain underexamined in the context of small retail POS systems. Mendoza and Cruz (2024) studied the implementation of credit control mechanisms in small business software applications and found that automated credit blocking, which prevents new credit sales to customers with existing unpaid balances, significantly reduces the incidence of bad debt without adversely affecting customer relationships. The study recommended that small business POS systems incorporate configurable credit policies that enforce credit limits and blocking rules, thereby removing the subjective element of credit approval from daily operations. This recommendation directly informs the design of the VISTA POS Module, which will implement a non-negotiable credit blocking rule that prevents any customer with an unpaid balance from making additional credit purchases.

**BIR Compliance and Official Receipt Generation**

The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) requires all registered businesses in the Philippines to issue official receipts for every sales transaction, as mandated by Section 113 of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC) and further detailed in Revenue Regulations No. 18-2012. Failure to issue proper receipts constitutes a violation subject to penalties under Section 264 of the NIRC, including fines ranging from PHP 1,000 to PHP 50,000 per violation and potential criminal prosecution for repeat offenders. Despite these requirements, compliance remains uneven among MSMEs, particularly in the agricultural retail sector where many businesses continue to use manually written receipts that lack the sequential numbering and detailed transaction information required by BIR regulations (BIR, 2023).

The distinction between VAT-registered and non-VAT registered businesses is a critical consideration in the design of receipt generation systems. Under Philippine tax law, businesses with annual gross sales exceeding PHP 3 million are required to register for VAT and must issue VAT invoices that separately state the VAT amount, while businesses below this threshold may register as non-VAT taxpayers and issue simplified receipts. The VISTA POS Module will implement a VAT-ready transaction structure that will accommodate both VAT and non-VAT scenarios, allowing the system to be configured according to the tax registration status of Villon Farm Supply. This approach follows the recommendation of Reyes and Tan (2023), who advocated for the inclusion of flexible tax configuration in POS systems designed for Philippine MSMEs, noting that many small businesses transition between VAT and non-VAT status as their revenue fluctuates around the registration threshold.

Digital official receipt generation and record retention have been the subject of recent BIR issuances, including Revenue Regulations No. 11-2018, which recognized the validity of electronically generated receipts provided they meet specific requirements for content, sequential numbering, and tamper-proof storage. The regulation requires that electronic receipts contain the business name, address, tax identification number (TIN), transaction date, itemized description of goods or services, amount, and a unique receipt number. The VISTA POS Module will comply with these requirements through its automated receipt generation feature, which will produce receipts in the OR-YYYY-XXXX format containing all mandatory fields. Furthermore, the system will maintain a complete digital record of all transactions, satisfying the BIR requirement that businesses retain transaction records for a minimum of ten years from the date of transaction.

**Software Quality and Technology Acceptance Models**

The evaluation of POS system quality requires a comprehensive framework that addresses both technical performance and user experience dimensions. The ISO/IEC 25010:2023 standard, which replaced the earlier ISO/IEC 9126 model, provides a structured approach to evaluating software product quality across eight characteristics: functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability. Al-Qutaish (2023) applied the ISO 25010 framework to the evaluation of retail POS systems and found that the model provides a holistic assessment that captures both the developer's perspective on software construction quality and the user's perspective on operational quality. The study recommended that POS system evaluations consider all eight characteristics in an integrated manner, rather than focusing exclusively on functional suitability, which is the most commonly evaluated dimension.

Technology acceptance models provide complementary frameworks for understanding the factors that influence user adoption and sustained use of information systems. The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), originally proposed by Davis (1989), posits that perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness are the primary determinants of user acceptance. The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology 2 (UTAUT2), developed by Venkatesh et al. (2012), extends TAM by incorporating additional constructs including social influence, facilitating conditions, hedonic motivation, price value, and habit. UTAUT2 has been widely adopted in information systems research as a more comprehensive model for predicting technology acceptance, particularly in contexts where users may have limited prior experience with the technology being evaluated.

Studies applying these frameworks to POS systems have yielded valuable insights. A study by Chen and Cheng (2023) applied UTAUT2 to evaluate the acceptance of cloud-based POS systems among small restaurant owners in Taiwan and found that performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and facilitating conditions were the strongest predictors of behavioral intention to use, while social influence and hedonic motivation had weaker effects in the business context. In the Philippine setting, a study by Dela Cruz and Villanueva (2024) used UTAUT2 to assess the acceptance of a mobile POS application among sari-sari store owners in Metro Manila and found that perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness were the most significant factors influencing adoption, while price value was a significant barrier given the limited financial resources of the target users. These findings inform the evaluation approach of this study, which will employ UTAUT2 to measure user acceptance, perceived ease of use, and perceived usefulness as efficacy of the developed VISTA POS Module.

**CHAPTER 3**

**METHODOLOGY**

**3.1 Research Design**

This study will use a descriptive-developmental research design. The rationale for this choice stems from the structure of the four statements of the problem, which collectively require the study to both describe an existing situation and build something in response to it. SOP1 requires a descriptive account of the current manual sales practices at Villon Farm Supply — specifically the five problems (S1 through S5) that the proposed system will address. This description cannot be assumed or inferred; it must be gathered directly from the people who operate the store. SOP2 requires development: translating those documented requirements into working software features. SOP3 and SOP4 then require evaluation of the developed system through ISO 25010:2023 and UTAUT2 respectively. The descriptive-developmental design is the only design that supports all four of these objectives within a single methodological framework.

The strength of this design, in the context of this study, is the way the descriptive findings shape what gets built. Rather than designing a POS module based on general retail software conventions, the VISTA POS Module will be designed around the five specific, documented problems found at a real store: a receipt book with no digital backup, an informal credit ledger that loses records, a missing OR numbering system, no credit blocking mechanism, and no automated daily sales summary. Each of these findings from SOP1 maps directly onto a specific feature in SOP2. This problem-to-feature traceability is only possible because the descriptive and developmental phases are methodologically unified — which is precisely what the descriptive-developmental design provides.

**3.2 Locale of the Study**

The study will be conducted at Villon Farm Supply in Tacurong City, Sultan Kudarat, Philippines. Tacurong City is a first-class municipality and the commercial center of the province, with an active agricultural trading community that supports a range of farm supply businesses. At the customer-facing level, Villon Farm Supply currently operates without a digital transaction system of any kind. Sales are written into a receipt book by hand, GCash and bank transfer payments are noted informally, and credit customers are tracked through a separate ledger that has no formal connection to the sales record. Sundays, which coincide with the local market day, are the store’s busiest day and generate the highest volume of transactions — precisely when the limitations of the manual receipt system are most exposed. The store’s BIR registration status means it is required to issue official receipts for every transaction, a requirement it cannot currently meet through its handwritten process.

**3.3 Respondents and Sampling**

The respondents for this study will be the Manager and Owner of Villon Farm Supply, selected through purposive sampling. The rationale for selecting these two individuals is grounded in their specific, non-interchangeable roles in the store’s current sales operations. The Manager is the person standing at the counter: writing receipts by hand, accepting GCash payments, maintaining the credit ledger, and deciding whether to extend further utang to a customer with an existing balance. The Manager’s firsthand experience with all five identified problems (S1 through S5) makes this individual indispensable for the requirements gathering phase and the primary evaluator of the system’s functional suitability and usability. The Owner, while not involved in daily transaction processing, holds financial oversight responsibility and will use the POS Module’s read-only transaction history and credit account views to monitor sales performance and receivables. The Owner’s evaluation perspective is therefore distinct from the Manager’s and necessary for a complete assessment of the module’s value. No other individuals at the store possess both the operational knowledge and the user role qualifications that these two respondents bring to the study.

**3.4 Research Instruments**

This study will utilize two primary research instruments to evaluate the developed VISTA POS Module. The first instrument will be an ISO 25010:2023 software quality evaluation form, which will assess the extent of compliance of the POS Module across the eight quality characteristics defined by the standard: functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability. The evaluation form will consist of Likert-scale items (1 = Not Compliant, 2 = Partially Compliant, 3 = Substantially Compliant, 4 = Fully Compliant) for each sub-characteristic of the ISO 25010 model. This instrument corresponds directly to SOP3 and will provide a structured, standards-based assessment of the technical quality of the developed system.

The second instrument will be a UTAUT2-based survey questionnaire, which will measure the level of acceptability and usability of the POS Module from the users' perspective. The questionnaire will address two primary dimensions: User Acceptance, which will encompass constructs such as performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and behavioral intention; and Perceived Ease of Use and Usefulness as Efficacy, which will assess the users' subjective evaluation of the system's usability and its contribution to task performance. The questionnaire will employ a five-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, 5 = Strongly Agree) and will correspond directly to SOP4. Both instruments will be validated by experts in information systems and educational technology prior to data collection to ensure content validity and reliability.

**3.5 Data Gathering Procedure**

The data gathering procedure will follow a systematic, multi-phase approach. In Phase 1, the researchers will conduct an initial assessment of Villon Farm Supply's current sales operations through observation and unstructured interviews with the Manager and Owner. This phase will document the existing manual sales recording practices, credit management procedures, and BIR compliance processes, providing the data needed to address SOP1. In Phase 2, the researchers will analyze the identified problems and requirements to develop detailed software specifications for the VISTA POS Module. This phase will translate the findings from Phase 1 into functional and non-functional requirements, which will address SOP2.

In Phase 3, the VISTA POS Module will be developed according to the specifications established in Phase 2, following an iterative development methodology that will incorporate regular feedback from the Manager and Owner. In Phase 4, the completed POS Module will be deployed at Villon Farm Supply for a testing period, during which the respondents will use the system in their actual work environment. In Phase 5, the respondents will evaluate the system by completing the ISO 25010:2023 evaluation form and the UTAUT2 survey questionnaire. The researchers will also conduct post-evaluation interviews to gather qualitative feedback on the system's strengths, weaknesses, and areas for improvement. **3.6 Data Analysis Plan**

The data analysis plan for this study will employ different analytical approaches for each statement of the problem, reflecting the distinct nature of the data collected for each research question. For SOP1, which seeks to identify the practices, issues, and problems encountered in the existing manual POS system, the data will be analyzed using thematic analysis. The interview transcripts and observation notes will be coded and categorized into recurring themes, which will be organized according to the five identified problem areas (S1 through S5) and any additional issues that emerge from the data. The thematic analysis will provide a rich, qualitative description of the current operational context and will serve as the foundation for the requirements specification in SOP2.

For SOP2, which identifies the specific requirements and interventions needed to address the current problems, the data will be analyzed using the same thematic approach as SOP1, with the findings structured as a requirements specification document that maps each identified problem to its corresponding software feature or intervention. This mapping will ensure traceability from problem identification to solution implementation and will facilitate validation of the system's design against the original requirements.

For SOP3, which evaluates the extent of compliance of the developed POS Module based on ISO 25010:2023, the data will be analyzed using weighted mean. Each ISO 25010 characteristic and sub-characteristic will be scored by the respondents on the four-point Likert scale, and the weighted mean for each characteristic will be calculated using the formula: WM = Σ(fx) / N, where f represents the frequency of each rating, x represents the rating value, and N represents the total number of respondents. The weighted mean scores will be interpreted using the following scale: 1.00 to 1.75 = Not Compliant, 1.76 to 2.50 = Partially Compliant, 2.51 to 3.25 = Substantially Compliant, and 3.26 to 4.00 = Fully Compliant.

For SOP4, which measures the level of acceptability and usability of the developed POS Module using UTAUT2, the data will likewise be analyzed using weighted mean. Each UTAUT2 construct will be scored on the five-point Likert scale, and the weighted mean for each dimension will be calculated. The weighted mean scores will be interpreted using the following scale: 1.00 to 1.80 = Strongly Unacceptable, 1.81 to 2.60 = Unacceptable, 2.61 to 3.40 = Neutral, 3.41 to 4.20 = Acceptable, and 4.21 to 5.00 = Strongly Acceptable. The use of weighted mean for both SOP3 and SOP4 provides a consistent quantitative methodology for evaluating the system's technical quality and user acceptance.

**3.7 Ethical Considerations**

This study will adhere to the ethical principles and guidelines established by Republic Act No. 10173, also known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012, which governs the collection, processing, and storage of personal information in the Philippines. Prior to data collection, the researchers will obtain informed consent from all respondents, clearly explaining the purpose of the study, the nature of their participation, the types of data that will be collected, and their right to withdraw from the study at any time without penalty. All personal information collected during the study, including the names, contact details, and business data of the respondents, will be treated as confidential and will be accessible only to the research team.

The data collected through the ISO 25010:2023 evaluation form and the UTAUT2 survey questionnaire will be anonymized prior to analysis to ensure that individual responses cannot be attributed to specific respondents. The research data will be stored securely in password-protected digital storage and will be retained only for the duration necessary to complete the study and any required follow-up analyses. Upon completion of the study, all raw data containing personally identifiable information will be permanently deleted in accordance with the data retention and disposal provisions of RA 10173. The researchers will also ensure that the development and testing of the VISTA POS Module will not disrupt the normal business operations of Villon Farm Supply, and the system will be deployed only with the explicit consent and cooperation of the business owner and manager.
