**Chapter 1**

**INTRODUCTION**

**1.1 Background of the Study**

Every season, small agricultural supply stores in developing economies absorb losses they cannot fully explain and shortages they did not anticipate. The problem, more often than not, is not a lack of products — it is a lack of information. Research on SME inventory practices in developing economies consistently documents that reliance on manual stock tracking leads to inaccurate records, delayed reorder decisions, and compounded losses from product spoilage — losses that are especially acute when the products in question carry expiry dates, as is the case with pesticides, seeds, and animal feeds (Alam et al., 2024). For agricultural retailers, a product that expires on the shelf represents capital lost twice: once when it was purchased, and again when it must be discarded without generating any revenue. The global push toward digital inventory management has been, in large part, a response to exactly this kind of preventable loss — a recognition that businesses operating without real-time stock data cannot make sound decisions about what to order, when to order it, or when to act before goods are wasted (Rosales et al., 2018). Large enterprises addressed this problem decades ago through enterprise resource planning systems and specialized inventory software, but small agricultural retailers in developing countries are still largely navigating by paper and memory.

In the Philippines, small agricultural retailers have been slow to make this shift toward digital inventory management. The Philippine Statistics Authority (2023) reports that micro and small enterprises account for over 99% of registered businesses in the country, with a significant portion still maintaining stock records in physical notebooks or ledger books. The agricultural supply sector faces a particular version of this challenge: seasonal demand spikes compress the window for restocking decisions, perishable products penalize late reorder actions more severely than shelf-stable goods do, and the farmers who depend on these stores operate on their own seasonal rhythms that do not wait for a business to catch up. When a farm supply store runs out of insecticide during planting season, the consequences reach beyond the store’s revenue and into the livelihoods of the farmers it serves. These are not edge cases — they are structural risks for any agricultural retailer that manages inventory without real-time data.

These are the conditions at Villon Farm Supply, a locally operated agricultural retail business in the Philippines. The store manages its stock through a physical record book that the manager updates by hand, and the primary method for verifying stock levels is a weekly full physical count that takes one to three hours to complete. Seven distinct inventory problems have been identified through a requirements analysis of the business: stock on hand is recorded only in a manual ledger with no digital counterpart (I1); the weekly count is time-consuming and error-prone (I2); there is no single screen showing current levels, alerts, and stock movement together (I3); expiry dates for pesticides, seeds, and feeds are not tracked in any systematic way (I4); the total monetary value of stock on hand is not known at any given moment (I5); stockouts have occurred when demand spikes outpaced the manual replenishment cycle (I6); and there is no way to distinguish fast-moving from slow-moving products across seasons (I7). Taken together, these seven problems describe a business navigating its inventory without instruments.

The VISTA Inventory Module is the proposed response to these seven problems. Positioned as the central hub of the Villon Integrated Supply and Trade Application, the module will receive stock data from the Purchasing Module each time goods arrive and pass deduction data to the POS Module each time a sale is recorded — keeping inventory counts current in real time without requiring the manager to touch a ledger. The module will provide a real-time stock dashboard, automated low-stock alerts, expiry date monitoring, First-In-First-Out (FIFO) costing, live inventory valuation, shrinkage recording, and predictive stockout estimation. Each of these features is a direct answer to one or more of the identified problems, and the module as a whole is designed to bring stock visibility to a business that currently has none.

What makes this study worth pursuing beyond its immediate scope is that the inventory problems at Villon Farm Supply are not unique to this business. Small agricultural retailers throughout Mindanao and across the Philippines share the same combination of seasonal demand variability, perishable stock, thin margins, and absent digital infrastructure. A purpose-built inventory module that runs on a standard Windows desktop, operates fully offline, and requires no enterprise-grade hardware or recurring subscription fees demonstrates that practical solutions to these problems can be built, evaluated, and documented at the small-business scale. The contribution of this study lies in establishing that proof — and in providing a replicable technical and methodological reference for researchers and developers who address similar challenges in the future.

**1.2 Conceptual Framework**

The conceptual framework of this study is built around the gap between what Villon Farm Supply currently knows about its own inventory and what it needs to know to manage it well. Three variable clusters define this framework. The first consists of the independent variables — the seven documented manual inventory problems (I1 through I7) and the practices that produced them: physical record books, weekly manual counts, the absence of expiry monitoring, and the absence of real-time valuation. The second cluster consists of the intervening variables — the design and development of the VISTA Inventory Module, which will translate the identified requirements into a working set of software features. The third cluster consists of the dependent variables — the outcomes the module is expected to produce: real-time stock visibility, automated alerts before stockouts occur, accurate FIFO-based valuations at any point in time, and reduced reliance on memory and manual labor for day-to-day inventory decisions. The relationship among these three clusters forms the conceptual foundation of this study.

The Output column represents what the completed system will deliver: a store where the manager opens a single screen and can see every product’s current quantity, its valuation contribution, its low-stock status, and any approaching expiry dates — all without touching a ledger. **1.4 Significance of the Study**

**Business Owners** stand to gain the most from this study. At present, the owner of Villon Farm Supply does not know the total monetary value of the stock on hand at any given time — problem I5 in the identified list. This is not a minor gap: inventory is the single largest asset of a merchandising business, and not knowing its value means making purchasing, pricing, and financial decisions without a key number. The VISTA Inventory Module will surface this figure in real time. Beyond valuation, the module will give business owners a view of which products are approaching low-stock thresholds, which have expiry dates coming up, and how total stock value has moved over time — enabling a level of financial oversight that is simply not possible when inventory is managed through a physical ledger.

**Store Managers** will feel the operational difference most directly. The weekly manual stock count — which currently consumes one to three hours of the manager’s time and is acknowledged to contain errors — will no longer be necessary as the primary source of stock data. Because the Inventory Module will update quantities automatically each time goods are received or a sale is recorded, the dashboard will reflect actual stock at all times. The manager will receive low-stock alerts before products run out rather than discovering shortages during a count, and the expiry tracking feature will create visibility into which batches of pesticides, seeds, and feeds are approaching their expiry dates before they become a write-off problem. The practical effect is a shift from reactive counting to proactive monitoring.

**Agricultural Retailers** throughout the Philippines who face the same inventory conditions will benefit from this study as a documented reference case. The combination of problems at Villon Farm Supply — seasonal demand, expiry-sensitive products, no valuation data, and no digital infrastructure — describes a common operating environment across the sector. This study will produce evidence that a purpose-built, offline-first desktop application can address all of these problems without requiring cloud services, internet connectivity, or expensive hardware. Retailers in similar positions will be able to consult this study’s findings, feature design decisions, and evaluation results as a starting point for their own digital inventory initiatives.

**Future Researchers** in information systems and agricultural technology will find this study useful as a methodological reference for evaluating inventory software in small-business contexts. The dual evaluation approach — ISO 25010:2023 for technical quality and UTAUT2 for user acceptance — applied to a module that addresses both FIFO costing and expiry date tracking simultaneously is a relatively underexplored combination in the literature. The evaluation results will provide baseline compliance and acceptance scores that future researchers can use for comparison, and the limitations of the current study — particularly the single-business scope and the exclusion of barcode scanning — will point toward productive directions for follow-on research.

**Software Developers** building inventory systems for small businesses will find value in the technical and design decisions documented in this study. The VISTA Inventory Module is built with Visual Basic .NET 10 on a WPF desktop framework, using an offline-first SQLite database that syncs to a centralized MariaDB instance when a stable connection is confirmed. This architecture prioritizes reliability under intermittent connectivity, which is a real constraint for businesses in the communities that agricultural retailers serve. Developers will also find the batch-level FIFO implementation and the expiry date tracking design — which must work together to alert on products that are both low in stock and near their expiry date — to be a technically interesting and practically relevant pattern for similar systems targeting agricultural or perishable-goods retail.

**1.5 Scope and Delimitation**

This study will focus on the development and evaluation of the Inventory Management Module of the Villon Integrated Supply and Trade Application (VISTA). The scope of the module encompasses the following core inventory management operations: a real-time stock dashboard displaying current stock levels, status indicators, and key performance indicators for all products; a product catalog for managing product information including category, pricing, and minimum stock thresholds; low-stock alerts that automatically trigger when stock falls below the defined minimum threshold; expiry date tracking for products with limited shelf life such as pesticides, seeds, and feeds; First-In-First-Out (FIFO) costing for accurate cost-of-goods-sold computation; inventory valuation that calculates the total monetary value of all stock on hand at any point in time; and shrinkage recording to document discrepancies between expected and actual stock levels. The module will support two user roles: the Manager, who will have full access to all inventory features, and the Owner, who will have read-only access to stock levels and product information.

The participants of this study will be the Manager and the Owner of Villon Farm Supply, who will serve as the respondents for both the requirements gathering phase and the evaluation phase. The study will be conducted at Villon Farm Supply, which carries approximately 50 stock-keeping units (SKUs) across four product categories: Fertilizers, Pesticides and Chemicals, Seeds, and Animal Feeds. The evaluation of the module will employ the ISO 25010:2023 Software Quality Standards and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology 2 (UTAUT2) as the primary assessment frameworks.

This study will be delimited to the Inventory Management Module only and will not cover the other modules of VISTA, including the Purchasing Module, the Point-of-Sale (POS) Module, and the Accounting Module. The module will be developed as a Windows Presentation Foundation (WPF) desktop application using VB.NET 10 with MariaDB or SQLite as the database, following an offline-first architecture. The study will not include mobile platform development, web-based deployment, or barcode and RFID scanning integration, as these features are planned for future versions of the application. Additionally, the evaluation will be limited to the two respondents from Villon Farm Supply and will not include a broader sample of agricultural retailers.

**1.6 Definition of Terms**

For the purpose of clarity and consistency, the following terms used throughout this study are defined as they will be employed in the context of the VISTA Inventory Module and the inventory management operations of Villon Farm Supply.

**Stock on Hand** refers to the current quantity of a product physically available in the store at any given time. This figure will be automatically updated by the system whenever goods are received through the Purchasing Module or sold through the POS Module, ensuring that the displayed quantity always reflects the most recent transaction.

**Minimum Threshold** is the predefined stock level set by the store manager for each product, below which the product will be flagged as low stock. This threshold serves as the trigger point for the low-stock alert system and is intended to prompt timely replenishment before the product reaches zero stock.

**FIFO Costing** (First-In-First-Out Costing) is an inventory valuation method that assumes the oldest units in stock are sold first. Under this method, the cost of goods sold is calculated based on the purchase price of the earliest acquired units, while the remaining inventory is valued at the cost of the most recently acquired units. FIFO is particularly appropriate for agricultural supply businesses where product expiry is a concern, as it aligns with the physical flow of goods.

**Inventory Valuation** is the total monetary value of all goods currently held in stock, computed as the sum of the stock on hand multiplied by the respective unit cost for each product. The VISTA Inventory Module will calculate this value in real time, providing the business owner with an instant snapshot of the capital invested in inventory at any moment.

**Low Stock** is a stock status indicator assigned to a product when its current stock on hand is at or below the minimum threshold but has not yet reached zero. Products in low-stock status will be highlighted with an amber visual indicator and will appear near the top of the stock dashboard to draw immediate attention to items requiring reorder.

**Out of Stock** is a stock status indicator assigned to a product when its stock on hand reaches zero, meaning the item is no longer available for sale. Out-of-stock items will be highlighted with a red visual indicator and will appear at the very top of the stock dashboard to ensure they receive the highest priority for replenishment.

**Shrinkage** refers to the loss of inventory that can be attributed to factors such as theft, damage, spoilage, or administrative errors. The VISTA Inventory Module will include a shrinkage recording feature that allows the manager to document and track these losses, enabling the business to identify patterns, quantify losses, and implement corrective measures.

**Expiry Date Tracking** is the systematic monitoring of the expiration dates assigned to products with limited shelf life, such as pesticides, seeds, and animal feeds. The module will record batch-level expiry dates and will generate alerts when products approach their expiration, allowing the business to prioritize the sale or disposal of near-expiry items and prevent the sale of expired goods to customers.

**Batch-Level Records** are inventory records that track stock at the level of individual purchase batches rather than aggregate quantities alone. Each batch record will include the quantity received, the unit cost at the time of purchase, the date of receipt, and the expiry date if applicable. Batch-level records are essential for implementing FIFO costing and expiry date tracking accurately.

**MerchSys.Inventory** is the designated namespace within the VISTA application architecture that encompasses all classes, data structures, and user interface components related to the Inventory Management Module. This namespace isolates inventory-related functionality from the other modules of VISTA, ensuring modular code organization and maintainability.

**Inventory Management Systems for Small Retail Businesses**

Small retail businesses have long struggled with the limitations of manual inventory management, which often leads to inaccurate stock records, delayed reorder decisions, and significant financial losses due to stockouts and overstocking. The challenges are particularly acute in developing economies where the adoption of digital inventory tools remains low due to cost barriers, limited technical expertise, and inadequate internet infrastructure. According to Alam et al. (2024), small and medium enterprises in developing countries frequently rely on paper-based inventory tracking methods that are vulnerable to human error, data loss, and inefficiency. These manual systems provide no real-time visibility into stock levels, making it difficult for business owners and managers to respond promptly to changes in demand or supply conditions. Digital transformation in inventory management addresses these gaps by introducing automated tracking, real-time visibility, and decision-support tools that reduce manual error and improve operational responsiveness (Rane et al., 2024).

The EzStock inventory management system, developed by researchers at Universiti Utara Malaysia (UUM), represents a notable effort to address the inventory management needs of small businesses (Lim et al., 2025). EzStock was designed as a lightweight, user-friendly application that enables small retail operators to track stock levels, generate purchase orders, and monitor product movement without the complexity and cost associated with enterprise-grade ERP systems. The study found that small businesses using EzStock experienced improved stock accuracy and reduced instances of stockouts, supporting the argument that purpose-built inventory software can deliver meaningful operational benefits even at a small scale. The design philosophy of EzStock — prioritizing simplicity, affordability, and ease of adoption — aligns closely with the approach taken in the development of the VISTA Inventory Module, which similarly targets a single small business with a focused feature set.

Digital transformation in inventory management has been shown to yield significant improvements in operational efficiency and decision-making quality for small retailers. A comprehensive study published on ResearchGate (Muller, 2024) examined the impact of digital inventory systems on small business performance and found that businesses that transitioned from manual to digital inventory tracking experienced a 25% reduction in stockout incidents and a 30% improvement in inventory record accuracy on average. The study emphasized that real-time visibility into stock levels is the single most impactful capability that digital systems provide, as it enables proactive inventory management rather than reactive responses to stock-related crises. These findings reinforce the importance of the real-time stock dashboard feature in the VISTA Inventory Module, which will provide Villon Farm Supply with instant access to current stock data for the first time.

**Real-Time Stock Monitoring and Alert Systems**

Real-time stock monitoring represents a fundamental shift in how retail businesses manage their inventory, moving from periodic manual checks to continuous automated tracking that provides up-to-the-minute visibility into stock levels, movements, and status. The benefits of real-time stock dashboards have been well documented in the literature. Jonsson and Mattsson (2023) found that real-time inventory visibility reduces the bullwhip effect in supply chains, enabling more accurate demand forecasting and more efficient replenishment cycles. For small businesses, where the margin for error in inventory decisions is particularly narrow, real-time monitoring can be the difference between maintaining adequate stock levels and experiencing costly stockouts that drive customers to competitors.

Automated low-stock alerts represent one of the most immediately impactful features of digital inventory systems, as they transform the process of reorder detection from a manual, error-prone task into a proactive, system-driven mechanism. When stock falls below a predefined threshold, the system automatically generates a visual or notification-based alert that prompts the manager to initiate a purchase order. Research by Syntetos et al. (2022) demonstrated that automated reorder point systems can reduce stockout rates by up to 40% compared to manual monitoring approaches, primarily because they eliminate the human delay and oversight that frequently cause reorder points to be missed. In the context of Villon Farm Supply, where stockouts have been identified as a recurring problem (I6), the implementation of automated low-stock alerts will directly address the root cause by ensuring that reorder signals are generated and received without relying on the manager's memory or routine manual checks.

Predictive stockout estimation extends the concept of real-time monitoring by using historical sales data and consumption patterns to forecast when a product is likely to run out of stock. This capability allows businesses to anticipate shortages before they occur and to plan replenishment activities accordingly. Wang and Hu (2023) developed a predictive stockout estimation model for retail inventory systems that incorporates seasonal demand variation, lead time variability, and safety stock calculations to generate probabilistic stockout forecasts. Their research showed that businesses using predictive stockout estimation achieved a 15% improvement in service levels compared to those relying solely on current-stock monitoring. For Villon Farm Supply, where seasonal demand fluctuations during palay planting and growing seasons significantly affect product turnover, predictive stockout estimation will provide a valuable forward-looking dimension to inventory management.

Expiry date tracking is a critical inventory management function for businesses that carry perishable or time-sensitive products, as it prevents the sale of expired goods, minimizes waste, and protects customer safety. In the agricultural supply sector, products such as pesticides, seeds, and animal feeds have defined shelf lives that must be monitored systematically to ensure product efficacy and regulatory compliance. Kelle and Woosley (2021) emphasized that expiry date tracking is not merely a regulatory requirement but a financial imperative, as the cost of disposing of expired inventory represents a direct loss of capital. The VISTA Inventory Module will implement batch-level expiry date tracking that records the expiration date of each received batch and generates alerts when products approach their expiry, addressing Problem I4 directly and ensuring that Villon Farm Supply can proactively manage its perishable inventory.

**FIFO Costing and Inventory Valuation Methods**

The choice of inventory costing method has significant implications for financial reporting, tax liability, and operational decision-making. The three most commonly used costing methods are First-In-First-Out (FIFO), Last-In-First-Out (LIFO), and Weighted Average Cost. Under the FIFO method, the cost of goods sold is calculated based on the purchase price of the oldest units in inventory, and the remaining inventory is valued at the cost of the most recently acquired units. Under LIFO, the opposite assumption applies: the newest units are assumed to be sold first. Under the Weighted Average method, a blended average cost is computed by dividing the total cost of goods available for sale by the total units available for sale. Each method produces different results in periods of changing prices, with FIFO generally yielding higher reported inventory values and lower cost of goods sold during periods of rising prices (Horngren et al., 2021).

FIFO is widely regarded as the most appropriate costing method for agricultural supply businesses for several reasons. First, the physical flow of goods in agricultural retail naturally follows a FIFO pattern: older stock is sold before newer stock to minimize the risk of products expiring on the shelf. This alignment between the physical flow and the cost flow assumption makes FIFO the most theoretically consistent method for this industry. Second, FIFO produces inventory valuations on the balance sheet that closely approximate current replacement costs, providing business owners with a more realistic picture of the capital invested in inventory. Third, in the context of Villon Farm Supply, where products such as pesticides and seeds have finite shelf lives, FIFO costing encourages the sale of older stock first, which complements the expiry date tracking feature and supports the overall objective of minimizing waste and loss (Warren et al., 2022).

The importance of knowing inventory valuation in real time cannot be overstated for small business owners who need to understand the financial health of their operations at any given moment. Without real-time inventory valuation, business owners are forced to make decisions based on incomplete or outdated information, which can lead to over-purchasing, under-stocking, or inaccurate financial reporting. The VISTA Inventory Module will calculate the total inventory value as the sum of the stock on hand multiplied by the unit cost for each product, providing Villon Farm Supply with an instant and continuously updated valuation that addresses Problem I5 directly. This real-time valuation capability will transform what is currently an unknown figure into an accessible and actionable business metric.

Batch-level costing is essential for accurate FIFO implementation, as it requires tracking the cost of each individual purchase batch separately rather than using a single blended cost for all units of a product. Under batch-level costing, each batch record includes the quantity received, the unit cost at the time of purchase, the date of receipt, and the expiry date if applicable. When a sale occurs, the system deducts units from the oldest batch first, calculating the cost of goods sold based on that batch's unit cost. This approach ensures that the FIFO assumption is applied precisely at the batch level, producing accurate cost-of-goods-sold figures and inventory valuations. The VISTA Inventory Module will implement batch-level costing to ensure that both the FIFO costing and the inventory valuation features produce financially accurate results that Villon Farm Supply can rely upon for decision-making and reporting purposes (Kieso et al., 2023).

**Software Quality Evaluation Using ISO 25010 and UTAUT2**

The International Organization for Standardization's ISO/IEC 25010:2023 standard provides a comprehensive framework for evaluating software product quality across eight characteristics: Functional Suitability, Performance and Efficiency, Compatibility, Usability, Reliability, Security, Maintainability, and Portability. This standard replaces the earlier ISO/IEC 9126 and introduces updated quality characteristics that reflect the evolving demands of modern software systems. Functional Suitability assesses the degree to which the software fulfills its stated functional requirements; Performance and Efficiency evaluates response times, resource utilization, and capacity; Compatibility examines the software's ability to operate in different environments and coexist with other systems; Usability measures the ease with which users can learn, operate, and navigate the software; Reliability assesses the system's ability to operate without failure under specified conditions; Security evaluates the protection of data and resources from unauthorized access; Maintainability measures the ease of modifying and extending the software; and Portability assesses the ease of transferring the software to different environments (ISO, 2023).

The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology 2 (UTAUT2), developed by Venkatesh et al. (2012), extends the original UTAUT model by incorporating additional constructs that are particularly relevant to consumer technology acceptance, including hedonic motivation, price value, and habit. For the purpose of evaluating the VISTA Inventory Module, the UTAUT2 framework will be adapted to focus on two primary constructs: User Acceptance, which measures the overall willingness of the respondents to adopt and continue using the developed system, and Perceived Ease of Use and Usefulness as Efficacy, which assesses the respondents' perception of how easily they can interact with the system and how effectively it improves their inventory management capabilities. The UTAUT2 framework has been widely validated in technology acceptance research and has been applied in numerous studies evaluating software systems in small business and agricultural contexts (Dwivedi et al., 2019).

The combined use of ISO 25010 and UTAUT2 in this study provides a dual-lens evaluation approach that addresses both the technical quality of the developed software and the human factors that determine its adoption and sustained use. This approach draws from well-established evaluation frameworks in the existing literature. Susanto and Al-Obaidi (2023) demonstrated the applicability of ISO 25010 in evaluating web-based information systems, confirming that quality characteristics such as usability and functional suitability are particularly critical in organizational contexts where end users have varied levels of technical proficiency. Dwivedi et al. (2019), in their comprehensive re-examination of the UTAUT framework, validated the theoretical constructs that underpin UTAUT2 and confirmed that performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and social influence remain the most consistent predictors of technology adoption across diverse settings. Together, these frameworks provide the present study with a theoretically grounded and empirically supported basis for evaluating both the quality and the acceptability of the VISTA Inventory Module.

**Chapter 3**

**METHODOLOGY**

**3.1 Research Design**

This study will follow a descriptive-developmental research design. The choice of this design is driven by the structure of the four statements of the problem, each of which requires a different mode of inquiry within a single integrated research effort. SOP 1 calls for description — documenting what actually happens when the manager of Villon Farm Supply tries to track inventory manually, including the specific practices behind the seven identified problems. SOP 2 calls for development — taking those documented requirements and translating them into the design and construction of the VISTA Inventory Module. SOP 3 requires systematic technical evaluation of the built product against the eight quality characteristics of ISO 25010:2023. SOP 4 requires a user-centered assessment of whether the respondents find the completed module acceptable and usable, measured through the UTAUT2 framework. The descriptive-developmental design is the only design that accommodates all four of these objectives coherently within a single study.

What justifies pairing descriptive and developmental methods in this study is what each phase contributes to the other. The descriptive phase is not simply a precondition for development — it produces specific, operational findings about the duration of weekly physical counts, the nature of ledger update errors, the frequency of stockouts, and the absence of expiry data that the development phase must directly address. Without this grounding, the module’s features risk being generic rather than targeted. Conversely, the developmental phase gives the descriptive findings a practical destination: the problems are not merely catalogued but resolved. This bidirectional relationship between problem identification and solution building is a defining characteristic of applied information systems research (Terziovski, 2020), and it ensures that the resulting module will be directly responsive to the actual inventory management conditions at Villon Farm Supply.

**3.2 Locale of the Study**

The study will be conducted at Villon Farm Supply, a single-location agricultural retail store in the Philippines that serves farmers and agricultural workers in its community. The store stocks approximately 50 distinct products across four categories: fertilizers, pesticides and chemicals, seeds, and animal feeds. What makes this store a particularly relevant context for an inventory study is the product mix itself — it contains both non-perishable items and products with defined expiry dates, including pesticides, seeds, and animal feeds, that must be monitored at the batch level to avoid selling degraded goods or absorbing expiry write-offs. Stock demand at the store is not uniform throughout the year; the palay planting and growing season drives sharp increases in demand for fertilizers, pesticides, and seeds, and Sundays attract the highest transaction volume of the week due to the local market. The store’s current inventory system — a single physical record book maintained by the manager — has no mechanism to anticipate these demand spikes or flag products approaching expiry.

Villon Farm Supply was selected for this study because it presents all seven inventory problems the module is designed to address, making it possible to evaluate the full feature set of the VISTA Inventory Module in a single site. The product mix, which spans both shelf-stable and expiry-sensitive goods, is specifically well-suited for evaluating the expiry date tracking and FIFO costing features, since a store carrying only non-perishable products would not generate the full range of inventory scenarios the system needs to handle. The store’s compact scale — approximately 50 SKUs and two primary personnel — also makes it feasible to complete a thorough development and evaluation cycle within the scope of this study without encountering the organizational complexity that would accompany a larger operation.

**3.3 Respondents and Sampling Techniques**

The respondents for this study will be the Manager and the Owner of Villon Farm Supply, for a total of two respondents. These two individuals are selected not simply because they are the business’s primary personnel, but because they represent the two distinct relationships that the VISTA Inventory Module must serve. The Manager is the person who currently performs inventory work — updating the physical record book, conducting the weekly stock count, and deciding when to reorder based on observed shelf levels. The Manager’s involvement in requirements gathering is essential because the seven identified problems originated from the Manager’s direct experience with the manual system, and the Manager will be the daily operator of the completed module. The Owner, by contrast, does not handle inventory records directly but has the most to gain from the module’s valuation and alert features: problem I5 — the unknown total monetary value of stock on hand — is experienced as a financial blind spot from the Owner’s perspective, and the study must verify that the module resolves it.

Both respondents will be selected through purposive sampling, which is the appropriate technique when participants must satisfy specific criteria tied directly to the research objectives (Creswell & Creswell, 2023). The criteria here are straightforward: respondents must have firsthand knowledge of the inventory operations at Villon Farm Supply, must have experienced the problems the module is designed to address, and must be the actual end users who will evaluate the completed system. Only the Manager and the Owner satisfy all three criteria. The resulting sample of two is consistent with the nature of this study, which focuses on a single small business rather than a cross-organizational population. The limitation this places on statistical generalizability is acknowledged, but it is offset by the depth and operational relevance of the evaluation data that these two respondents — as the only people who work directly with the inventory system being replaced — are uniquely positioned to provide.

**3.4 Research Instruments**

Two research instruments will be used in this study to evaluate the VISTA Inventory Module. The first instrument is the ISO 25010 Compliance Evaluation Form, which is a researcher-made questionnaire designed to measure the extent of the developed module's compliance with the eight quality characteristics of the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 standard: Functional Suitability, Performance and Efficiency, Compatibility, Usability, Reliability, Security, Maintainability, and Portability. The questionnaire will use a five-point Likert scale, where 1 indicates strongly disagree and 5 indicates strongly agree, and will include multiple items for each quality characteristic to ensure comprehensive coverage. The weighted mean of the responses for each characteristic will be computed and interpreted using the following scale: 1.00 to 1.80 as strongly disagree, 1.81 to 2.60 as disagree, 2.61 to 3.40 as neutral, 3.41 to 4.20 as agree, and 4.21 to 5.00 as strongly agree.

The second instrument is the UTAUT2 Acceptability Survey, which is a modified version of the UTAUT2 questionnaire originally developed by Venkatesh et al. (2012). This survey will measure the level of acceptability and usability of the developed inventory module from the perspective of the end users, focusing on two primary constructs: User Acceptance and Perceived Ease of Use and Usefulness as Efficacy. The survey will also use a five-point Likert scale and will be modified to align with the specific context and features of the VISTA Inventory Module, ensuring that the items are relevant and understandable to the respondents. Both instruments will undergo content validation by a panel of experts prior to deployment, and Cronbach's alpha reliability testing will be conducted to ensure the internal consistency and reliability of each instrument, with a threshold alpha value of 0.70 or higher considered acceptable.

**3.5 Data Gathering Procedure**

The data gathering procedure for this study will follow a systematic seven-step process. In the first step, the researcher will secure the necessary permissions and informed consent from the Owner and Manager of Villon Farm Supply prior to conducting any data collection activities. In the second step, the researcher will conduct semi-structured interviews with both respondents to gather detailed information about their current inventory management practices, the problems they encounter, and their specific requirements for a digital inventory system. In the third step, the researcher will observe the current inventory operations firsthand, including the process of updating the physical record book, conducting manual stock counts, and making reorder decisions, to supplement the interview data with direct observational evidence.

In the fourth step, the researcher will analyze the data gathered from interviews and observation to identify the specific inventory problems (I1 through I7) and to derive detailed software requirements for the VISTA Inventory Module. In the fifth step, the researcher will develop the inventory module based on the identified requirements, following an iterative development process that incorporates feedback from the respondents at key milestones. In the sixth step, after the module has been fully developed and deployed in the operational environment of Villon Farm Supply, the researcher will administer the ISO 25010 Compliance Evaluation Form and the UTAUT2 Acceptability Survey to both respondents and will collect their responses for analysis. In the seventh and final step, the researcher will compile, tabulate, and analyze all collected data using the statistical methods described in the Data Analysis Plan, and will prepare the findings, conclusions, and recommendations of the study.

**3.6 Data Analysis Plan**

The data analysis plan for this study will employ both qualitative and quantitative methods corresponding to the four statements of the problem. For SOP number one, which asks about the practices, issues, and problems encountered in the existing manual inventory system, the researcher will use thematic analysis to identify, organize, and interpret the patterns and themes that emerge from the interview transcripts and observation notes. Thematic analysis will involve a systematic process of coding the data, identifying recurring themes, and constructing a coherent narrative that accurately represents the respondents' experiences and the operational realities of the current inventory management practices at Villon Farm Supply.

For SOP number two, which asks about the specific requirements and interventions needed to address the identified problems, the researcher will employ qualitative requirement specification techniques to translate the interview and observation findings into structured software requirements. This process will involve categorizing the identified problems, mapping each problem to a proposed feature or intervention, and documenting the requirements in a format that directly guides the development of the VISTA Inventory Module.

For SOP number three, which asks about the extent of compliance of the developed module with the ISO 25010:2023 Software Quality Standards, the researcher will compute the weighted mean of the respondents' ratings for each of the eight quality characteristics. The weighted mean will be calculated by multiplying each Likert scale response by its corresponding frequency, summing the products, and dividing by the total number of responses. The resulting weighted means will be interpreted using the five-point scale described in Section 3.4, with a significance level of alpha equal to 0.05. For SOP number four, which asks about the level of acceptability and usability of the developed module as measured by the UTAUT2 framework, the researcher will similarly compute the weighted mean for each UTAUT2 construct (User Acceptance and Perceived Ease of Use and Usefulness as Efficacy) using the same five-point Likert scale interpretation and the same significance level of alpha equal to 0.05.

**3.7 Ethical Considerations**

The participants will be informed of the purpose of the study, and to ensure ethics was taken into consideration, an informed consent form will be filled out. A consent will also be obtained prior to the interview. To ensure anonymity, all information will be treated with utmost confidentiality in accordance with RA 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012. The researcher will ensure that all data collected from the respondents — including interview transcripts, observation notes, and survey responses — will be stored securely and will be accessible only to the researcher and the research adviser. No personally identifiable information will be disclosed in the research report, and all data will be used exclusively for the purposes of this study. The respondents will also be informed of their right to withdraw from the study at any time without penalty, and the researcher will obtain explicit consent for the audio recording of interviews prior to each session.
